Caffeine, Sleep, and Training: Dose the Benefit Without Trashing Recovery

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Afternoon coffee helps the tempo — and quietly steals deep sleep you need to absorb that tempo. The conflict is acute performance versus next-day adaptation. Caffeine is a legal ergogenic aid with a long tail; treating it like water is how runners become “mysteriously” bad sleepers.

Half-Life Timing

Average caffeine half-life is often cited around ~5–6 hours (wide individual range). That means a chunk of a 3 p.m. dose is still active near a 10–11 p.m. bedtime. Sensitive runners feel sleep onset delay from doses most people call “fine.” If sleep scores and morning RHR drift while training load is stable, move caffeine earlier before you blame the plan. Sleep context: sleep and running performance.

Worked Scenario: 70 kg Runner, 10 p.m. Bedtime

Habitual intake ~150–200 mg by noon. Race literature often discusses ~3–6 mg/kg for performance in tolerant athletes → roughly 210–420 mg total — but that is not a dare. Practical plan:

  • Training days: keep total moderate; last dose ~8–10 hours before bed when possible (for a 10 p.m. bedtime, think ~noon–2 p.m. cutoff)
  • Hard afternoon session: small top-up only if you already tolerate it; do not add a huge late dose “for this workout”
  • Race morning: use the dose you practiced in long runs or tune-up races — not a double shot you saw on social media

Keep easy runs easy even when caffeinated — use the Zone 2 Calculator so stimulants do not turn recovery days into gray-zone junk.

Expert Note

Half-life varies with genetics, pregnancy, medications, and liver function. “3–6 mg/kg” performance ranges assume healthy adults who already use caffeine; non-users can feel jitters, GI upset, or HR spikes at lower doses. Caffeine is not a substitute for fitness or carbs. If you have cardiac symptoms, anxiety disorders, or clinician limits on stimulants, follow medical advice over race folklore.

Evening Decision Rule

If sleep has been fragile for several nights, cut afternoon caffeine before you cut training quality. If a late meeting “requires” coffee, accept a lower-intensity next morning rather than stacking poor sleep plus intervals. Race week: freeze your caffeine protocol by mid-week — no new energy gels with surprise caffeine loads.

FAQ

Should I quit caffeine before a race?

Usually no — abrupt withdrawal can cause headaches. Keep your practiced pattern; do not escalate the dose dramatically on race morning.
