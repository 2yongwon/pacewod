Running Recovery: Rest, Sleep, Nutrition, and Easy Days

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Training breaks you down; recovery builds you back. Prioritize rest, sleep, fueling, and honest easy days — not stacking hard session on hard session.

Rest and Sleep

Take at least one full rest day weekly (walking OK; skip the “recovery jog” if the body wants off). After long runs: rest or very easy. After intervals: easy only for 24–48 hours. Sleep 7–9 hours; keep a consistent schedule, cool dark room, limited late caffeine, and 2–3 hours after evening runs before bed.

Nutrition That Speeds Bounce-Back

  • Within 60 min post-run: ~3:1–4:1 carb-to-protein
  • Daily protein: 1.4–1.8 g/kg
  • Fluids: replace ~150% of sweat loss after long runs

Estimate burn with the Calories Calculator — under-fueling is a common recovery failure.

Easy Days Done Right

Recovery running only works at true easy effort. Cap HR with the Zone 2 Calculator or follow the easy run pace guide. Active options: 20–30 min walk, easy bike/swim, mobility, 5–10 min foam rolling.

When to Back Off (and What Wearables Mean)

Unplanned rest if: resting HR is +5 bpm above baseline, heavy legs 3+ days, illness, or worsening localized pain. Garmin Body Battery / Training Readiness / HRV Status and COROS recovery timers are directional — not vetoes. Trust 7-day RHR vs 30-day baseline (+3–5 bpm for 2+ days matters). HRV: five low nights with rising RHR beats one yellow icon. Local Achilles pain overrides a green score. Set zones in the HR Zones Calculator.

Practical Takeaway

Morning checklist before quality work: RHR (±2 bpm green; +5 for 2+ days red), HRV trend, sleep (7+ hours green; under 6 red), soreness (1–3 OK; 7–10 or unilateral stop). Two yellow flags or any red → rest or walk. Stick to one watch ecosystem for a training block so trends stay comparable.

FAQ

Recovery jog or complete rest?

If RHR is within ~2 bpm of baseline and legs are stiff not painful, 20–30 min zone 2 can help. If RHR is +5+, sleep was poor, or illness is brewing — rest.

DOMS or injury?

DOMS is bilateral, diffuse, peaks 24–48 hours, then improves. Injury is often unilateral, localized, and worsens under load — stop and rest at least 72 hours.
