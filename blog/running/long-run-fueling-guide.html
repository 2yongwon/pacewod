Long Run Fueling: Carbs, Electrolytes, and GI Strategy for 90+ Minute Runs

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Past ~90 minutes, glycogen — not fitness — often causes pace fade and rising HR at the same effort. Trained runners store roughly 1,500–2,000 kcal of glycogen (about 90–120 minutes of moderate work). Mid-run carbs keep the last hour as training, not survival.

Carb Targets Per Hour

  • 75–90 min: 20–30 g/hr
  • 90–120 min: 30–45 g/hr
  • 120–180 min: 45–60 g/hr
  • Marathon race pace: 60–90 g/hr if the gut is trained

Add 10–15 g/hr every 2–3 long runs. Formats: gels (~20–25 g), chews (20–30 g), sports drink (14–20 g per 8 oz), or real food on easy/ultra efforts. Estimate burn with the Running Calories Calculator.

Timing and Pre-Run Setup

Start fueling at 30–45 minutes — do not wait for hunger. Sample 2-hour plan at 45 g/hr: gel at 30, 60, and 90 minutes with water. Set a 30-minute watch alert. Pre-run: 1–3 g/kg carbs 2–3 hours out (oatmeal, toast, banana); keep fat/fiber low; 16–20 oz fluid beforehand.

Fluids and Sodium

  • Cool: ~12–16 oz/hr; sodium often optional under 90 min
  • Moderate heat / 90–150 min: 16–24 oz/hr + 300–600 mg sodium/hr
  • Hot / heavy sweaters: up to ~28 oz/hr + 600–1,000 mg sodium/hr

Weigh before/after (dry clothes): ~1 lb lost ≈ 16 oz fluid. Replace ~150% of losses over 4–6 hours with sodium, not water alone. Over-drinking plain water risks hyponatremia.

Train the Gut

Practice race products 8–12 weeks out. Increase carbs gradually; test gels at goal pace; chase gels with 4–8 oz water. If GI hits: slow 5–10 minutes, sip water, skip stacking another gel. Half long runs: 30–45 g/hr. Marathon long runs: build 45–60+ g/hr and simulate aid-station rhythm.

Practical Takeaway

Fuel early at 30–60 g carbs/hr (scale up with duration), pair fluid with sodium in heat, and rehearse until race day is boring. Pair with the Long Run Guide and Pace Calculator.

FAQ

Do I need fuel under 90 minutes?

Usually no under 75–90 minutes if you ate beforehand. Exceptions: fasted starts, heat, or hard segments — one gel mid-run is low-risk insurance.

How do I avoid hitting the wall?

Eat 30–60 g/hr from minute 30, start conservatively, and arrive glycogen-topped. Late-run HR drift at stable pace often means under-fueling, not lost fitness.
