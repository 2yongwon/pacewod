How to Improve VO2 Max

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Improving VO2 max means raising the ceiling of oxygen delivery and use — then expressing it as faster interval and race paces. Gains come from hard aerobic intervals plus enough easy volume to recover and adapt.

Know Your Zones First

Set easy and hard ranges with the Zone 2 Calculator, HR Zones Calculator, and race-based paces via the Pace Calculator / Race Predictor. Estimate current fitness with the VO2 Max Calculator.

Methods That Work

  • Long VO2 intervals: 5 × 3 min hard / 2–3 min jog
  • 800s: 5–8 × 800m @ 3K–5K, jog recoveries ~equal to work or slightly less
  • Billat 30/30: short hard/easy switches to accumulate time near VO2
  • Hill repeats: 60–90 s hard climbs — high cardiac load, lower impact speed
  • Zone 2 base: supports recovery and raises the floor under VO2 work

Sample Week

  • TueVO2 intervals
  • ThuEasy or light strides
  • SatOptional tempo OR second quality (advanced)
  • OtherEasy Zone 2 + long run

Expect noticeable interval-pace changes in ~4–8 weeks if easy days stay easy. Genetics and age cap absolute VO2; economy and threshold still move race times.

Watch Estimate Limits

Garmin/COROS VO2 can stall or drop from heat, trails, or wrong max HR even when fitness rises. Trust race/workout evidence over a single estimate tick.

Mistakes That Cap Gains

  • Gray-zone easy days that blunt interval quality
  • Chasing watch VO2 daily instead of workout evidence
  • Stacking VO2 + long tempo + long run in 72 hours
  • Recoveries too short so rep pace collapses by #3

If interval pace at the same RPE is not improving after 6–8 weeks, fix sleep, easy-day compliance, and progression — not “more intensity every day.”

FAQ

How often should I do VO2 intervals?

Usually once per week in a 4–8 week block. Twice only if volume is high and easy days remain truly easy.

Practical Takeaway

One focused VO2 session per week, protected by honest easy mileage, beats random hard running. Progress the workout (reps, pace, or recovery) every 1–2 weeks — not every session.
