Running in the Rain: Gear, Safety, and Pace Adjustments

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Wanting to prove toughness and needing safe footing often conflict in a downpour. Rain is trainable; lightning and flooded roads are not “mental strength.”

Gear and Visibility

  • Light waterproof/breathable shell over a moisture-wicking base; avoid cotton
  • Hat with brim; clear/amber lenses if needed; reflective gear in low light
  • Shoes with usable tread — slick road racing flats are optional misery

Related: hot weather, cold weather, Zone 2, Pace Calculator.

Numeric scenario: Planned easy 5:45/km. In steady rain with puddles and wind, expect ~10–30 sec/km slower at the same effort — run by HR/RPE. If you force goal pace and cadence collapses on slick corners, you traded training for a fall risk.

Expert Note

Wet clothes increase heat loss. In cool rain, shorter easy runs beat heroic long runs in soaked cotton. Change quickly after; don’t sit in wet kit.

Decision rule: thunder/lightning or standing water over curbs → treadmill, indoor bike, or move the session. No PR is worth that risk.

Pacing, Routes, and Aftercare

Choose well-drained routes; avoid flooded underpasses and metal surfaces. Shorten strides on slick corners and paint. In cold rain, consider splitting a long run or moving it indoors rather than collecting a deep chill. Afterward: dry socks/shoes, warm shower, and hot meal — especially if you still have evening training. Quality sessions in storms can move to treadmill with the treadmill pace caveats. Mental toughness is showing up smartly for months, not risking a fall for one wet tempo.

Carry a simple plan B: indoor bike, treadmill, or strength + easy row when storms are unsafe. Getting soaked on an easy day is optional character building; forcing intervals on flooded paths is poor risk math. Dry your watch and phone ports too — gear failures are part of wet-weather reality.

If you finish soaked and cold, prioritize dry clothes before scrolling your splits — cooling too long raises the cost of an otherwise easy run.

Practical Takeaway

Dress to stay visible and warm enough, slow to effort, and cancel for lightning. Consistency includes smart substitutions.

FAQ

Will rain ruin my shoes?

They’ll get wet — air dry them, remove insoles, and rotate pairs. Don’t bake them on a heater.
