Running in the Rain: Gear, Safety, and Pace Adjustments

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Rain rarely ruins training — poor gear choices and rigid pace targets do. Stay visible, keep traction, and run by effort when roads are slick. Light rain is fine for most easy and tempo work; lightning, flood risk, or black ice are hard stop rules. Finished wet miles still count toward weekly volume when you stay honest with effort.

Gear That Helps

  • Shell: light water-resistant jacket beats a fully waterproof sauna for steady runs; vent when you overheat
  • Layers: synthetic or wool base; skip cotton
  • Feet: shoes with decent tread; thin wool socks; optional gaiters in downpours
  • Visibility: bright/reflective kit + light in low cloud; drivers see worse in rain
  • Extras: hat/visor for eyes; lotion/anti-chafe where wet fabric rubs

Cold rain steals heat faster than dry cold — dress one notch warmer than the thermometer suggests. See also cold weather running.

Safety First

  1. No outdoor running with thunder/lightning — treadmill or delay.
  2. Avoid flooded underpasses and fast sheet flow; standing water hides holes.
  3. Shorten stride on paint, metal plates, leaves, and wet wood.
  4. Face traffic; assume you are invisible at dusk.

Pace and Effort Adjustments

Wet roads and heavier clothes often cost 5–20+ sec/mile at the same effort. Cap intensity with HR/RPE from the Zone 2 Calculator and HR Zones — not dry-day GPS. Convert adjusted targets in the Pace Calculator.

  • Easy days: hold conversational effort; ignore “slow” splits
  • Intervals: shorten reps or add recovery if footing is sketchy
  • Long runs: prioritize continuous time-on-feet over pace PRs
  • Race day rain: more conservative early pace (see race-day pacing)

Practical Takeaway

Dress to manage wet and cold, stay visible, and train by effort when traction drops. Finished rainy miles still count. Related: Hot Weather Guide, Treadmill vs Outdoor.

FAQ

Will rain ruin my shoes?

They get wet and dry. Stuff with paper, air-dry away from direct heat, and rotate pairs if you run daily in storms.

When should I move inside?

Lightning, flooding, ice, or when quality intervals need reliable footing — protect the stimulus on the treadmill.
