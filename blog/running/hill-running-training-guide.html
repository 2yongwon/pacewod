Hill Running Training Guide

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Hills raise intensity without forcing track speed: more force per stride, high cardiac load, and race-specific strength. Used well, they build power and confidence; used poorly, they trash calves and turn easy days into gray-zone climbs.

Grade and Effort

Pace slows uphill at the same effort — judge hills by RPE/HR, not flat pace. Pair signals using RPE vs Heart Rate. Convert race goals with the Pace Calculator and Race Predictor, then expect grade-adjusted slowing on routes.

Key Hill Sessions

  • Short power (10–20 s, ~8–12%): near-max, full walk-down recovery — neuromuscular power
  • VO2 hills (60–90 s, ~5–7%): hard controlled, jog down — aerobic power
  • Long strength (2–4 min, ~4–6%): strong aerobic, steady form
  • MP hill blends: long-run segments at marathon effort over rolling grade

Downhill Matters

Races are often lost on descents. Slight forward lean from the ankles, quick light steps, avoid overstriding brakes. Add short controlled downhill strides before racing steep courses; soreness is eccentric — progress gradually.

Weekly Fit and Mistakes

  • Replace one flat quality day with hills — don’t stack hills + track + long hard weekend
  • Easy days: walk steep pitches to stay Zone 2
  • Treadmill: use incline for specific sessions; 1% is not a “hill workout”
  • Common errors: too much grade too soon, sprinting every hill on long runs, ignoring downhill eccentrics

Trail vs Road Hills

Trail grades are irregular and demand more ankle/foot stability; shorten sessions when surfaces are technical. Road hills give repeatable grade for measured reps. Either way, easy trail runs still need Zone 2 honesty — hike the steep pitches.

Treadmill Note

For VO2-style hills, 5–8% at hard effort with easy jog or walk recoveries works. Keep “easy” treadmill days near 0–2% and Zone 2 HR — steep incline every day is not base training.

Practical Takeaway

Program hills with a purpose (power, VO2, or strength), recover like any hard session, and keep easy runs honest on rolling terrain. Hills are a tool — not a reason for every run to become a climb contest.

FAQ

Hills instead of track VO2?

Yes as a substitute block, especially if impact tolerance is limited. Keep one flat speed touch before short races if possible.
