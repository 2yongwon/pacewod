Marathon Pacing Strategy: Even Splits, Fueling Checkpoints, and the Second Half

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Mile 1 in 7:45, mile 20 in 8:30, mile 26 in 10:15 — the classic positive split. Sixty seconds per mile fade over the last 10K adds minutes you cannot get back. Marathon pacing is honest goal setting, even (or slight negative) splits, and non-negotiable fueling checkpoints.

Set Pace Honestly

Anchor with the Race Predictor from a recent half or marathon-specific training — not a sharp 5K alone. Convert with the Pace Calculator and Pace Chart. Prefer even splits or a tiny negative; large early surpluses rarely stick.

Checkpoints and the First-Mile Trap

Adrenaline makes goal pace feel effortless in mile 1. Hold back ~10–15 seconds per mile for the first 2–3 miles, then lock goal pace. Key cumulative checks at 10K, half, 30K / 20 miles. If you are far ahead at halfway, you did not “bank time” — you spent it.

Weather and hills: widen acceptable bands on climbs; accept higher HR in heat without chasing pace. Adjust the goal before the start if the forecast is hot or the course is net hilly.

Fueling Checkpoints

Fuel every ~30 minutes or ~every 5 miles, starting by mile 5 — whichever comes first. Target ~45–60 g carbs/hour (up to ~90 g/hr if gut-trained). Skipping the mile 18–21 gel window is a common cause of late collapse. Set watch alerts; treat fueling as mandatory regardless of how you feel early.

Miles 20–26.2

Protect form and cadence. If pace slips despite rising effort, stop surging — hold the best sustainable rhythm and keep taking carbs/fluids. The second half is management, not discovery.

Practical Takeaway

Even splits + practiced fueling beat a heroic first half. Rehearse marathon-pace segments and gels on long runs. Race the cumulative plan you printed, not the crowd in mile two.

FAQ

Even or negative splits?

Even or slight negative. Large negative splits usually mean you sandbagged fitness; large positive splits usually mean you opened too fast or underfueled.

How often should I fuel?

About every 30 minutes / every 5 miles from mile 5 onward, at ~45–60 g carbs/hour (higher if trained). Don’t skip late gels because you “feel fine.”
