Zone 2 Based on LTHR vs Max HR: Which Method to Use

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Same athlete, same “Zone 2” label — different methods can disagree by 15–20+ bpm. Max HR describes a ceiling; LTHR marks where aerobic work starts costing more. For easy-day discipline, threshold-anchored zones usually match physiology better.

Two Reference Points

% max HR (often 60–70% of max, sometimes from 220−age) is simple but loose. LTHR (e.g. Friel running Zone 2 ≈ 85–89% of LTHR) targets true base intensity below rapid lactate accumulation.

Max-HR Zone 2 often sits lower in absolute bpm than LTHR Zone 2. Follow the low range and easy runs feel artificial; follow a high range without a real threshold and “easy” becomes moderate.

Same Athlete, Different Numbers

Example: age 38, max HR 186, resting 52, LTHR 168:

  • 220−age (60–70%): ~109–127 bpm
  • Measured max (60–70%): ~112–130 bpm
  • Karvonen 60–70%: ~132–146 bpm
  • Friel LTHR 85–89%: ~143–150 bpm

At 145 bpm, LTHR says Zone 2; age-max says Zone 3+. Compare your methods in the Zone 2 Calculator and HR Zones Calculator.

Garmin Defaults

  • Out of the box: often % max, Zone 2 = 60–70%, max = 220−age until you fix it
  • Supports LTHR / %HRR — switch basis in Connect if you train by threshold
  • Auto-LTHR can lag fitness jumps or skew after heat/hills — treat as draft

When to Test LTHR

Worth it if you train by HR ≥3 days/week, watch “easy” conflicts with feel, or your max is far from age norms. Field option: 30-min hard TT; average HR of last 20 min ≈ LTHR. Retest every 6–8 weeks in a build. Running LTHR ≠ bike LTHR.

Why LTHR Zone 2 Can Exceed 70% Max

If LTHR is a high fraction of max HR (common in well-trained runners), 85–89% of LTHR can sit above 70% of max. That is expected — not a bug. It means % max charts were too conservative for your physiology. Still verify with the talk test: if 148 bpm is conversational and sustainable, it can be true Zone 2 even when a default Garmin chart calls it Zone 3.

Practical Takeaway

  1. Enter a real max HR (hard effort), not 220−age.
  2. Prefer LTHR zones for committed HR-based easy days.
  3. Validate with talk test + chest strap on steady runs.
