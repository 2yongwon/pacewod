Half Marathon Pacing Strategy: First 10K Discipline and the Final 5K Push

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Mile 3 feels effortless; mile 10 feels like a different sport. The half marathon rewards restraint early and controlled aggression late. Most fades are written in the first 10K, not a mysterious “wall” at mile 11.

Set Goal Pace From Evidence

Use the Race Predictor and a recent 10K (or half) — not a hopeful stretch time. Convert with the Pace Calculator / Pace Chart. A 1:45 half typically aligns with roughly 47–48 minute 10K fitness. Goal pace should feel slightly too easy at mile 2. If you think “I could hold this forever” at 2 miles, you are probably right on plan.

Three Phases

  • First 5K: ~5–8 sec/km slower than even-split goal. Ignore the corral surge. Start slightly behind a pace group if needed.
  • 5K–10K: Lock the goal band. Longest hold segment. Check cumulative time, not every noisy GPS blip.
  • 10K–13.1: Progressive push only if 10K was near plan (example for 1:45: 10K ~49:30–50:15). Last 5K is earned, not assumed.

1:45 example (4:58/km): 5K 25:00–25:15 · 10K 49:45–50:15 · 15K 1:14:30–1:15:00 · 20K 1:39:30–1:40:00 · finish 1:44:45–1:45:15.

HR, Hills, Fueling

Pace leads; HR confirms. Expect ~5–10 bpm drift at constant pace after ~30 minutes — see zones via the HR Zones Calculator. On hills, hold effort and use cumulative checkpoints. Practice gels/fluids in long runs (often starting early in the race); late-race decisions assume early fueling happened. Hot days: shift decisions toward RPE and splits, not HR targets from cool training.

Race-Morning Execution

Eat the practiced breakfast, start slightly restrained for 3K, and check cumulative time at 5K and 10K before any surge. If GPS is noisy downtown, trust effort and mile markers more than live pace. A tune-up race or long-run segment at goal pace in the final weeks beats rewriting the plan on the start line.

Practical Takeaway

First 10K discipline creates the final 5K. Race evidence-based pace, not the crowd. Rehearse half-marathon pace segments in training so “correct” is familiar on race morning.

FAQ

Should I start with a faster pace group?

No. Match the group to your predictor-backed goal. Drafting helps; being towed 15–20 sec/mile fast in miles 1–3 usually costs more later.

What if I feel great at mile 6?

Hold goal pace through ~10K. Feeling great early is the plan working — not a green light to surge yet.
