Deload Weeks for Runners: When to Cut Volume and What to Keep

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

A deload is a planned down week so fitness can absorb. Skipping it feels tough until intervals go flat and niggles stack. The conflict is consistency culture vs recovery physiology. Deload ≠ race taper (tapering guide). Watch recovery context: HRV, overtraining signs, recovery guide. Keep easy days true with the Zone 2 Calculator; recalibrate with the Pace Calculator and HR Zones; related structure in polarized 80/20.

When to Schedule One

  • Every 3–4 weeks of progressive load for most recreational plans
  • After stacked quality, a race, or a breakthrough long-run block
  • When RHR/HRV/mood trend wrong for several days

Numeric scenario: Typical week 50 km with two quality sessions. Deload → 22–30 km (about −40–60%), keep 4–5 run days shorter, drop to one light quality touch: e.g., 5 strides or 15 minutes easy tempo — not 6 × 800. By midweek you should feel eager. If you’re still wrecked, extend easy days another 3–4 days before rebuilding.

Expert Note

Complete couch rest for seven days often leaves legs oddly heavy. Keep light movement and a whisper of speed; cut volume, not all neuromuscular contact.

Decision rule: if easy pace-at-fixed-HR is still worse than baseline at the end of a deload week, do not jump into a big interval set — add another easy stretch first.

What Success Looks Like

Sleep improves, resting HR settles, and the first quality session afterward feels snappy. Guilt is not a metric.

Strength training can continue at reduced volume: keep technique sets, skip max-effort PRs. Social pressure to “stay consistent” is why deloads fail — put the down week on the calendar like a race. Related easy-day structure in polarized plans still applies: most volume remains aerobic even when the week is short.

After the deload, resume with the next logical week — not a revenge mileage spike. If the first quality session back feels snappy, the deload worked.

Practical Takeaway

Plan deloads before your body forces one. Cut volume hard, keep a little sharpness, and protect easy intensity.

FAQ

Will I lose fitness in one deload week?

No — you lose fatigue faster than fitness. That is the point.
