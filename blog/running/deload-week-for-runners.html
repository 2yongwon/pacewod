Deload Weeks for Runners: When to Cut Volume and What to Keep

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

A deload is a planned down week so your body absorbs mileage and workouts. Skip them and you get flat intervals, rising resting HR, and niggles — not “toughness.” Done right, the next block starts fresher and faster.

When to Deload

  • Every 3–4 weeks of progressive load for most recreational plans
  • After a hard block (big long runs, race, or stacked quality weeks)
  • When Garmin/COROS recovery metrics, HRV, or mood trend down for several days — see HRV for runners and overtraining signs

Deload ≠ full taper. Race tapers are sharper and timed to an event (tapering guide).

Cut Volume 40–60%

Reduce weekly distance/time by roughly half. Shorten the long run most; keep frequency if possible (e.g., same days, shorter runs) so the habit stays. Easy days stay truly easy — use the Zone 2 Calculator.

Example: 50 km week → ~20–30 km deload. Drop one quality session or shrink both.

Keep Some Intensity — Lightly

Complete rest for a week often leaves legs “dead.” Keep a touch of speed: 4–6 strides, a short fartlek, or 15–20 min easy tempo — not a full interval set. Intensity reminds the nervous system; volume is what drops.

What Success Looks Like

By mid/late deload you should feel eager, not guilty. Resting HR and sleep often improve. Watch “training readiness” scores are noisy — trust legs and the next workout’s pace more than a single badge. Recalibrate effort with the Pace Calculator after the down week.

Practical Takeaway

Schedule deloads every 3–4 weeks or after hard blocks: cut volume ~40–60%, keep light intensity, and protect easy days. Related: recovery guide, polarized 80/20.

FAQ

Will I lose fitness in one deload week?

No — aerobic fitness is sticky. You lose fatigue faster than fitness; that is the point.

Can I strength train on a deload?

Yes at reduced volume. Keep technique work; skip new max-effort PRs.
