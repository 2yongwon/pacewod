Zone 2 Running: The Complete Guide

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Your watch calls the run Zone 2 at 5:40/km. Your breathing says Zone 3. That gap — not a textbook definition — is the whole problem with easy aerobic work. Zone 2 only pays off when the cardiac load stays low enough that you can stack volume without stealing tomorrow’s quality session.

Set a Ceiling Before You Chase Pace

Most runners treat Zone 2 as “a bit slower than usual.” Better: pick a heart-rate ceiling and treat pace as an output. Use the Zone 2 Calculator and cross-check methods in the HR Zones Calculator. LTHR-anchored Zone 2 often sits higher than % max HR defaults — see LTHR vs Max HR.

  • Talk test: full sentences without gasping
  • Sensor: chest strap beats wrist optical on hills
  • Pace: ignore it when heat, sleep, or hills raise cost

Worked Example: Same Athlete, Two “Easy” Days

Athlete: max HR 186, resting 54 → Karvonen Zone 2 ≈ 133–146 bpm. Cool flat Tuesday: 50 minutes at 5:50/km averaging 141 bpm, conversational — true Zone 2. Hot Thursday: same 5:50/km hits 155 bpm by mile three. Decision: slow to ~6:10/km or walk the hills until HR sits under 146. The slower day is still the better easy day.

Expert Note

Do not treat factory Garmin/COROS Zone 2 (often 60–70% of 220−age) as physiology. Age-formula ceilings can sit 15–20 bpm too low — or too high if your real max is above average. Recalibrate after a hard max effort or a field LTHR test, not after one sweaty long run.

Stay Honest After Minute 40

  1. Cap HR; walk hills on easy days.
  2. Prefer flat routes when the goal is aerobic volume.
  3. Expect 5–10 bpm drift after 60+ minutes at steady pace; more than about 10% drift → slow down (heat, fueling, or started too fast).

Progress marker: pace at the same Zone 2 HR improves over 4–6 weeks — not Training Effect badges. Related: Zone 2 vs Zone 3, Easy Run Pace Guide.

Decision Rule

If HR is above your Zone 2 ceiling while the plan says easy, slow or walk until it drops — even if pace looks “embarrassing.” If both talk test and HR pass, keep the pace you have.
