Zone 2 Running: The Complete Guide

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Zone 2 is easy aerobic running — conversational effort that builds the engine for every race distance. Most runners skip it by defaulting to moderate pace. Stay honest with heart rate, and easy days get faster at the same HR within weeks.

What Zone 2 Is

Typically ~60–70% of max HR, or ~60–70% of heart rate reserve (Karvonen). Body relies mainly on fat, builds mitochondria and capillaries, and accumulates little residual fatigue. Find your range with the Zone 2 Calculator; cross-check methods in the HR Zones Calculator.

  • Talk test: full sentences without gasping
  • Watch: chest strap > wrist optical for hills and intervals
  • Pace: ignore it on easy days — heat, sleep, and hills move cost

Why It Matters

  • Supports high weekly volume without crushing recovery
  • Improves fat oxidation and pace-at-fixed-HR over 4–6 weeks
  • Protects quality of interval/tempo sessions (polarized ~80% easy / 20% hard)

LTHR-anchored Zone 2 often sits higher than % max HR defaults — see LTHR vs Max HR. Age-formula Zone 2 can be 15–20 bpm too low for the same athlete.

How to Stay Honest

  1. Cap HR at your Zone 2 ceiling; walk hills if needed.
  2. Prefer flat routes on easy days.
  3. Treat Garmin/COROS time-in-zone cautiously — wrist HR lags 5–15 s; factory Zone 2 is often 60–70% of a wrong max HR.
  4. On runs >60 min, cardiac drift of 5–10 bpm at steady pace is normal; >10% drift → slow down (heat, fueling, or started too fast).

Sample Week

  • MonZone 2 — 45 min
  • WedIntervals (hard)
  • ThuZone 2 — 40 min
  • SatLong Zone 2 — 75–90 min
  • OtherRest or easy 30 min

Example: max HR 186, resting 54 → Karvonen Zone 2 ≈ 133–146 bpm. If easy pace at that HR improves from ~6:05/km to ~5:35/km over six weeks, the base is working — not Garmin Training Effect badges.

Practical Takeaway

Set a real Zone 2 ceiling, walk rather than creep into Zone 3, and track pace-at-fixed-HR monthly. Related: Zone 2 vs Zone 3, Easy Run Pace Guide.
