Running Cadence: Optimal Steps and Drills

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

“Everyone must hit 180” conflicts with real runners who race well at 168–175. Cadence is a tool against overstriding, not a religion. Measure it on easy runs with your watch, then nudge — don’t force a sudden +20 spm jump that creates calf overload. Pair form work with honest easy pace via the Pace Calculator, Zone 2 Calculator, and easy run pace guide.

What Cadence Actually Fixes

Low cadence often pairs with long ground contact and heel braking. Slightly quicker steps at the same pace usually shorten stride and reduce impact peaks — useful for shin and knee niggles when load is otherwise managed. Cadence is not a cure for doubling mileage in two weeks.

Numeric scenario: Easy pace 6:00/km at 160 spm. Raise metronome target to 168–170 spm (+5–6%) for 4 × 2 minutes inside an easy run, full easy cadence between. Over 3–4 weeks, if average easy-run cadence rises to ~168 and the same pace feels smoother (less “braking”), keep it. If calves scream for three days after every session, you jumped too far — cut the cue to +2–3%.

Expert Note

Cadence rises naturally when you speed up. Compare cadence at the same easy pace before/after drills. A race cadence of 180 with an easy cadence of 165 can be normal — don’t chase race cadence on recovery days.

Decision rule: change cadence by ≤5–8% at a time and only on easy days first. If pain increases (especially shins/calves) for >48 hours after cueing, revert and fix load/shoes/strength before another cadence push.

Drills Without the Gimmicks

  • Metronome or playlist BPM near target for short blocks
  • Quiet feet / “hot ground” cues for 20–30 seconds
  • Strides focusing on quick turnover, not sprinting

Strength still matters: weak hips and calves make cadence cues fail under fatigue. Recheck average cadence monthly on similar easy routes — not on every hilly surge day.

Practical Takeaway

Use cadence as a gentle anti-overstride lever at fixed easy pace. Progress slowly and judge by feel and niggle trends, not a magic 180 badge.

FAQ

Is 180 required?

No. Many efficient runners live in the high 160s to mid-170s at easy pace. Direction of change matters more than a single target.
