Running Cadence: Optimal Steps and Drills

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Cadence is steps per minute (spm). Most recreational runners overstride at low cadence; raising step rate toward 170–180 spm often shortens stride, lands feet under the hips, and cuts braking force — without requiring more speed.

The 170–180 Target

170–180 spm is a practical range for most distance runners (elites often exceed 180 at race pace). If you are under 165, raise cadence 5–10% over 4–6 weeks — jumping from 155 to 180 overnight loads calves and Achilles. Easy runs might sit near 170; 5K pace often near 180+. Avoid plodding below 165 on easy days.

Measure and Practice Without Speeding Up

Use GPS-watch cadence, a metronome at 170–175 bpm, or count footstrikes for 30 seconds × 2. Practice on easy days first: metronome segments inside a 30–40 minute easy run. Lock pace with the Pace Calculator or treadmill speed so higher spm does not become disguised tempo. Confirm zone 2 with the Zone 2 Calculator.

Example: Easy pace 6:10/km at 158 spm → over weeks hold ~9.7 km/h and climb toward 170–171 spm; stride length may drop ~8% (e.g., ~1.05 m to ~0.97 m). If HR climbs 8+ bpm at the same pace, back off 3–5 spm.

Drills (2–3×/week)

After an easy warm-up: 2–3 × 20–30 m of A-skips, B-skips, high knees, butt kicks; plus 4–6 × 80–100 m strides at 5K effort with quick light steps. Cadence drills do not replace strength — calf raises, hip abduction, and glute work support higher turnover.

Garmin / COROS Cadence Data

Both derive cadence from accelerometer peaks. Treadmill often reads 2–4 spm higher than overground. Wrist cadence is usually fine above ~8:00/mile steady; intervals can inflate spm by 5–8 from arm swing. Prefer lap or live cadence (±5 spm on flat terrain) over a post-run average that hides hills. Foot pods add ground-contact and vertical metrics beyond spm alone.

Practical Takeaway

Raise cadence gradually at fixed pace and HR. Economy shows up as better pace-at-same-HR over 4–6 weeks, not a forced 180 spm shuffle into zone 3. Pair with the easy run pace guide.

FAQ

Does cadence matter on hills?

Yes, but targets shift — uphill often 175–185 spm with shorter steps; downhill focus on quick light steps, not a flat 170 number.

Is 168 spm a problem if I am healthy?

Not if you are injury-free and efficient. Cadence is a form cue, not a mandatory scoreboard.
