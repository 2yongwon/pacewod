Overtraining Signs for Runners: RHR, HRV, Performance, and When to Rest

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Normal fatigue clears with a day or two easy. Functional overreaching is planned hard load with rebound after a deload. Non-functional overreaching and overtraining linger — performance drops, mood and sleep fray, and more easy miles do not fix it. Catch the slide early with simple trends.

Resting HR and HRV Trends

Track morning RHR against a 30-day baseline. +3–5 bpm for 2+ days with heavy legs or poor sleep → cut intensity. Pair with HRV: a single low night is noise; several low days with rising RHR is signal. See resting heart rate and recovery and HRV for runners. Set training zones with the HR Zones Calculator.

Performance and Subjective Red Flags

  • Easy pace requires higher HR / higher RPE for a week+
  • Interval or tempo paces slip despite “trying harder”
  • Persistent soreness, irritability, loss of motivation, frequent niggles
  • Sleep fragmentation, lingering illness, elevated resting HR

Load Mistakes That Cause It

Too many gray-zone days, doubles without easy discipline, stacking MetCons + hard running, skipping cutback weeks, and under-fueling. Keep most volume in zone 2 (Zone 2 Calculator) and protect 1–2 quality sessions — not five “kinda hard” ones.

Garmin, COROS, Apple Scores

Garmin Training Status “Overreaching,” low Body Battery/HRV Status, COROS fatigue flags, and Apple third-party readiness apps are prompts — confirm with RHR, sleep, and workout quality. One red icon after a race week can be fine; four weeks of declining performance plus autonomic flags means deload. Details: how to read Garmin Training Status.

Practical Takeaway

If two or more of RHR↑, HRV↓ trend, sleep↓, and performance↓ align for several days: rest or true easy only for 3–7+ days, fix fueling/sleep, then resume with lower intensity. Seek medical help for persistent collapse, chest symptoms, or suspected illness. Recovery is sleep, food, and patience — not another tempo test.

FAQ

Overreaching or overtraining?

Overreaching rebounds in days–weeks with rest. Overtraining lasts months and needs medical/coaching intervention — rare if you deload when early flags appear.

Should I do a recovery run when flags are red?

Only if RHR is near baseline and legs feel OK — keep it short zone 2. Otherwise full rest wins.
