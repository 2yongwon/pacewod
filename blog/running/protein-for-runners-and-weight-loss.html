Protein for Runners and Weight Loss: Grams Per Kilogram and Meal Timing

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Cut calories without enough protein and you often lose more than fat — paces soften, easy runs feel harder, strength fades. A controlled deficit with protein held high is how recreational runners keep training quality while body fat declines.

The 1.6–2.2 g/kg Target

During a cut, aim for roughly 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg body weight per day. Examples: 60 kg → ~95–130 g; 70 kg → ~110–155 g; 80 kg → ~130–175 g. Toward the higher end if you lift, run high volume, or are leaner already.

Distribute across 3–5 feedings (~20–40 g each) rather than one huge dinner. A day that works: ~30 g at breakfast, 30–40 g lunch, 20–30 g post-run snack if needed, 40 g dinner. Daily average beats perfect post-run shakes.

Timing Around Runs

  • Before hard sessions: include some carbs; a little protein is fine if tolerated
  • After hard/long runs: protein + carbs in the next meal/snack supports repair (see recovery nutrition)
  • Easy days: keep the daily total; timing is flexible

Protein helps satiety and lean-mass retention; it does not magically create fat loss without a deficit. Pair with the framework in calorie deficit for runners and session estimates from the Running Calories Calculator.

Sources and Mistakes

Meat, dairy, eggs, soy, fish, and mixed plant proteins all work if totals add up. Powder is optional convenience. Mistakes: tiny breakfast protein, “runners need carbs so protein can wait,” and trusting a single yogurt to cover a 150 g day.

Adjusting Over a Cut

As body weight drops, update the g/kg target every few kilograms. If training quality dips, raise carbs before cutting protein. If hunger is the limiter, push protein toward the top of the range and add volume foods (veg, broth-based meals) rather than slashing another 300 kcal overnight.

Practical Takeaway

If you track one metric in a cut, track weekly average protein. Hit 1.6–2.2 g/kg, keep easy runs easy (Zone 2 Calculator), and let the deficit — not extreme restriction — do the fat-loss work.

FAQ

Does more protein increase fat loss by itself?

Only indirectly via satiety and muscle retention. You still need a calorie deficit over time.

Is a post-run shake required?

No. Useful when a meal is delayed after hard/long runs. The weekly protein average matters more than the 30-minute timer.
