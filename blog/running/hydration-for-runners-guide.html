Hydration for Runners: Sweat Rate, Sodium, and Pre/During/Post Run Strategy

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Dehydration of ~2% body weight can raise HR and slow pace; over-drinking plain water without sodium risks hyponatremia. The fix is personal: measure sweat rate, then match fluid and sodium to conditions — not a generic “drink more.”

Sweat Rate Test

Weigh nude (or dry kit) before a 60-minute easy–moderate run. During: record fluid consumed. After: dry off, reweigh. Sweat loss (L) ≈ kg lost + liters drunk. Convert to ml/hr. Repeat in cool and hot weather — rates differ. Pair calorie/effort context with the Calories Calculator if useful, but sweat rate drives fluid math.

Fluid and Sodium Targets

  • During running: commonly ~400–800 ml/hr (≈14–27 oz), adjusted to sweat rate and thirst
  • Sodium: often ~300–700 mg/hr; heavy sweaters/heat may need more
  • Pre-run: 400–600 ml in the 2–3 hours before; pale-yellow urine as a rough check
  • Post-run: replace ~150% of body-mass loss over 4–6 hours with sodium-containing fluids/food

See also long-run fueling and hot-weather running for duration and heat specifics.

During-Run Strategy

Under ~60–75 minutes in cool weather, many runners need little beyond starting hydrated. Longer/hotter: sip on a schedule if thirst lags, carry fluid or loop past fountains, and include electrolytes when sessions exceed ~90 minutes or sweat is heavy. Cardiac drift at stable pace can partly reflect fluid deficit — check with the Zone 2 Calculator effort caps rather than forcing pace.

Hyponatremia Warning Signs

Watch for weight gain during a race, severe headache, nausea, confusion, or swollen hands after drinking large volumes of plain water. Prevent by matching intake to sweat losses and including sodium on long hot efforts — not by forcing maximum fluids.

Practical Takeaway

Test sweat rate, aim ~400–800 ml/hr as a starting band, add 300–700+ mg sodium/hr when you sweat hard, and rehydrate to ~150% of losses afterward. Build a written plan for long runs and races; adjust for heat index, not ego.

FAQ

Is urine color enough?

Useful pre-run screen, imperfect mid-race. Dark often means behind; clear plus weight gain can mean over-drinking.

Should I drink to thirst only?

Thirst is a good default for many; in long hot events, planned sipping plus sodium based on sweat rate is safer than pure guesswork or forced over-drinking.
