Running Doubles: Two-A-Day Training for Advanced Runners

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Doubles split daily volume into two runs (usually AM primary + PM easy) so you can raise aerobic minutes without one endless session. They work for advanced runners — not as a shortcut from 50 to 80 km/week.

When Doubles Make Sense

Prerequisites: 55–70+ km/week held 8–12 weeks, true easy-pace discipline, RHR within ~2–3 bpm of baseline, 7+ hours sleep, and time for a second run without cutting rest. Useful in marathon/ultra builds, heat (AM/PM cool windows), or schedules that cannot fit one long block. Skip if under ~50 km/week, returning from injury, quality sessions are already failing, or RHR is +5 bpm for multiple days. See the return-to-running guide.

Easy PM Run Rules

  • Length: 20–45 minutes (shorter after quality/long AM)
  • Intensity: conversational; lower half of zone 2 — use the Zone 2 Calculator
  • Gap: at least 4 hours (6–8 ideal)
  • Surface: flat/soft when possible; shuffle pace is correct

Never make the PM run a second hard workout. If you missed AM quality, do not “make it up” at night.

Keep 80/20 Across the Week

Doubles should grow the easy side of the week. Target ~75–85% easy time, under ~10% gray zone, 15–20% hard in 2–3 sessions. Cap at 1–3 double days/week (start with one). Sample ~70 km week: doubles Mon/Tue/Fri; Tue tempo + short PM; Thu intervals with no PM; optional short shuffle after Saturday long only if fresh. Framework: polarized 80/20 guide.

Recovery Between Sessions

Within 30 minutes of AM: 25–40 g protein + 40–60 g carbs. Sleep 7.5–9 hours. RHR +3 bpm one day after a hard double can be normal; +5 bpm for 2+ days → cut PM runs. Every fourth week, remove PM doubles and cut AM volume 20–30%.

Practical Takeaway

PM = shuffle, short, zone 1–low zone 2. Monitor morning quality paces, RHR, and sleep. If metrics trend wrong for 48+ hours, cut PM before cutting AM quality.

FAQ

How many doubles per week?

One to three in peak blocks. Confirm RHR and sleep stay stable for 2–3 weeks before adding another.

Should PM always be shorter?

Yes — typically 25–50% of AM duration. Easy-easy doubles of two short runs are the main exception.
