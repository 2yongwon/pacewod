Running Economy: Cadence, Strength, and Why VO2 Max Isn't Everything

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Two runners with similar VO2 max can race minutes apart. That conflict is running economy (RE): oxygen cost at a given pace. Improving RE means the same engine covers more ground. VO2 max still matters — see the VO2 max guide and VO2 Max Calculator — but economy is often the lever you can train without chasing a lab number.

Where Economy Comes From

  • Neuromuscular timing (cadence, stiffness, ground contact)
  • Strength and tendon stiffness from targeted gym work
  • Specificity: race-pace exposure without junk gray-zone volume

Numeric scenario: Runner A and B both show ~52 ml/kg/min estimated VO2. At 5:00/km, A’s steady HR is 148; B’s is 158 at the same pace and conditions. B is “less economical” in the field sense — same pace costs more cardiac load. Over 8 weeks, if B’s HR at 5:00/km falls to ~150 while VO2 estimate barely moves, economy improved even if the watch VO2 badge is flat.

Expert Note

Watch VO2 estimates mix pace and HR with proprietary models. Prefer field checks: pace at fixed easy HR, or HR at a fixed easy pace on similar terrain and weather. Don’t invent progress from a single outdoor vs treadmill comparison.

Decision rule: if easy pace at fixed Zone 2 HR is stuck for 4+ weeks while volume is stable, prioritize economy work (strides, strength, cadence) before adding another VO2 interval day.

Levers That Usually Move RE

  • Cadence: many recreational runners sit under 165 spm; nudging toward ~170–180 with shorter steps often reduces overstride — cadence guide
  • Strides: 4–8 × 20–30 s after easy runs for coordination without full interval fatigue
  • Strength: 2×/week hips, calves, single-leg work — strength for runners
  • Quality: short race-pace intervals once economy basics exist — intervals

Recheck with the Pace Calculator after a race or time trial, not after every easy run. Heat, hills, and poor sleep raise HR at the same pace — log conditions beside the number.

Practical Takeaway

Treat VO2 as ceiling and economy as cost-per-mile. Train the cost with cadence, strength, and smooth speed — then let intervals express the fitness you already built.

FAQ

Do lighter shoes automatically fix economy?

They can shave cost for some runners, but form and training usually dominate. Change one variable at a time.
