Running Economy Guide

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Running economy is how much oxygen (or energy) you use at a given pace. Two runners with the same VO2 max can race very differently if one wastes less energy each stride. Economy often decides marathon and half outcomes when aerobic ceilings are similar.

Economy vs VO2 Max

VO2 max is the size of the engine; economy is how far that fuel takes you. Improve either — or both. Background: VO2 Max Guide, estimates via VO2 Max Calculator. Garmin VO2 estimates partly reflect pace–HR efficiency, so they blur economy and VO2 — trend them, don’t equate them to a lab.

What Moves Economy

  • Aerobic volume: easy miles improve tissue and metabolic efficiency
  • Cadence: many recreational runners improve toward ~170–180 spm without forcing a sprint shuffle — see cadence guide
  • Strides: 6–8 × 20–30 s fast, relaxed, 2–3×/week — neuromuscular economy dose
  • Strength: 1–2×/week heavy-ish posterior chain and single-leg work — strength for runners
  • Some quality: controlled intervals/tempo teach efficient race mechanics — intervals

When Economy Beats Raw VO2

Long races, hot weather, late-race fade patterns, masters longevity, and 5K–10K matchups where threshold is equal. Track economy without a lab: pace at fixed easy HR, and race pace at sustainable RPE — use the Pace Calculator for anchors.

8-Week Focus Block

  • Keep polarized easy volume
  • Add strides after 2–3 easy runs/week
  • 2 strength sessions; one quality run (not both volume and intensity spikes)
  • Retest a fixed HR easy loop every 4 weeks

Common Economy Mistakes

  • Forcing cadence with tiny steps and tense shoulders
  • Skipping strength because “miles are enough”
  • Judging economy only by Garmin VO2 ticks
  • Doing daily strides to failure — keep them relaxed and short

Economy can improve while VO2 max is flat — that is still free speed at marathon and half effort.

FAQ

Can economy improve without VO2 max rising?

Yes. Strength, strides, and easy volume often lower energy cost at race pace while VO2 stays flat — that still improves performance.

Practical Takeaway

Don’t chase VO2 badges alone. Stack easy volume, strides, and strength — then use pace-at-HR as your economy scoreboard.
