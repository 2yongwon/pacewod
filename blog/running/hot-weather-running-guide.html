Hot Weather Running: Heat Acclimation, HR Drift, and Pace Adjustments

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Heat raises core temperature, cardiovascular strain, and dehydration rate. Same pace can sit 10–15+ bpm higher; spring targets in summer heat become junk miles. Acclimate and adjust pace — do not “tough it out.”

Heat Acclimation (10–14 Days)

Repeated heat exposure increases plasma volume, earlier/higher sweating, and lower HR at a given workload — most adaptations within 10–14 days. Days 1–3: 20–40 min easy in warmth, 30–60 sec/mile slower. Days 4–7: extend duration; keep intensity easy–moderate. Days 8–14: reintroduce quality cautiously, still slower than cool-weather paces. Miss several cool days and some adaptations fade within a week or two.

Pace and HR Drift Adjustments

Expect roughly 1–3% slower pace per ~5°F above 60°F, larger in humidity. Rule of thumb: heat index >80°F → HR often +10–15 bpm at same effort; >90°F → slow 20–45 sec/mile or train by HR/RPE. Cardiac drift rises faster in heat — hold zone 2 ceilings from the Zone 2 Calculator, not April splits. Convert targets with the Pace Calculator.

Hydration in Heat

Start hydrated; for long/hot runs use fluid + sodium (often ~16–28 oz/hr and 300–1,000 mg sodium/hr depending on sweat rate — see the hydration guide). Weigh before/after; replace ~150% of losses. Pale urine pre-run is a useful check, not a perfect lab.

Treadmill vs Outdoor + Stop Rules

Use treadmill or cooler windows for key workouts when heat index is extreme; keep some outdoor heat exposure for acclimation. Stop for dizziness, chills, cessation of sweating, confusion, nausea, or sharp chest symptoms — cool, hydrate, seek help if needed. Prefer early morning; light colors; wet sponge/ice when available.

Practical Takeaway

Acclimate 10–14 days, slow for heat index, cap effort by HR, and fuel/hydrate on purpose. Summer fitness is maintained by completed easy volume at honest effort — not by matching cool-weather paces.

FAQ

Why is my easy HR 12 bpm high?

Heat and humidity divert blood to the skin and raise HR at the same pace. Slow until you are back in zone 2; fitness is not gone overnight.

When should I move the workout inside?

When heat index is extreme, quality suffers, or safety feels marginal — protect the stimulus on the treadmill and keep shorter outdoor heat exposures for acclimation.
