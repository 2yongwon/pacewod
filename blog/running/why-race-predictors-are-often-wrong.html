Why Race Predictors Are Often Wrong

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Your Garmin says 3:35 marathon. You run 3:48. A fast 5K makes COROS and the PaceWOD Race Predictor look optimistic for the half. That gap is normal. Predictors estimate what recent data could support under favorable conditions — not a guaranteed finish time.

What Predictors Actually Model

Watches blend estimated VO2 max, recent races or time trials, training load, and HR–pace relationships. Formula tools (Riegel: T₂ = T₁ × (D₂/D₁)^1.06) extrapolate from one result to another. VO2 max sets an aerobic ceiling; threshold, economy, durability, and execution decide the race. Two athletes with the same VO2 max can finish minutes apart.

Why Longer Predictions Miss More

  • Riegel stretch: 5K→10K is usually reasonable; 5K→marathon assumes marathon-specific fitness you may not have. Many coaches add ~3–8% when anchoring a marathon off a short race. Prefer the closest comparable distance (half → marathon beats 5K → marathon).
  • Threshold & economy: Half and marathon live near sustainable pace for hours. Predictors infer threshold indirectly and rarely know if your last 10K was tapered or mid-build.
  • Mileage & durability: Same 10K PR at 35 vs 70 mpw is not the same marathon. Load metrics ≠ long-run specificity.
  • Environment: Heat, humidity, wind, altitude, and hills are usually outside the model. Hot dew-point marathons often cost 5–15 minutes vs cool flat predictions.
  • Fueling: Models assume carbs are available. Rough glycogen stores (~1,800–2,200 kcal) cover ~90–120 minutes hard — not a full marathon at goal pace without 30–60 g carbs/hour (up to ~90 g/hr if gut-trained).

Practical Takeaway

Use predictions as pacing guidance and A/B/C planning — more reliable for 5K/10K than half/marathon. Start near the projected range only if training matches the distance and conditions are good. Add conservatism for short-race anchors, thin long-run volume, heat, or untested fueling. Trends over months beat one optimistic number the week before.

FAQ

Why is my marathon predictor optimistic after a fast 5K?

Riegel-style math assumes you already have marathon durability and fueling. Without long runs and race-pace practice, spreadsheet times run hot — add conservatism or anchor off a half marathon.

Are watch predictors better than Riegel?

They use more training data, but still miss weather, hills, and race-day fueling. Treat both as ranges, not contracts.
