Resting Heart Rate and Recovery: What Garmin, COROS, and Apple Watch Track

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Morning resting heart rate (RHR) is still one of the most useful recovery signals. Paired with HRV and sleep, it tells you when to push quality — and when to protect it. Absolute numbers matter less than your baseline.

What RHR and HRV Indicate

RHR rises with illness, poor sleep, alcohol, heat, and accumulated training stress. Compare today’s value to a 7–30 day average, measured similarly (upon waking, before coffee). HRV (often RMSSD overnight) tends to fall when recovery is incomplete — use trends, not one morning. Context: HRV for runners.

Platform Differences

  • Garmin: resting HR, overnight HRV Status, Body Battery, Training Readiness — useful when sleep tracking is consistent; optical overnight data needs a snug fit
  • COROS: resting HR + HRV/recovery timers tied to training load; stick to one sleep window for trends
  • Apple Watch: strong resting HR history in Health; HRV via Breathe/sleep apps or third-party readiness (Athlytic, etc.) — standardize the app and timing

Do not hop brands mid-block and expect comparable scores. Wrist optical is noisier than a morning chest-strap seated test, but overnight consistency usually beats irregular perfect tests.

Decision Rules for Training

  • RHR within ±2 bpm of baseline: execute the plan
  • +3–4 bpm one day after a hard long run: monitor; keep easy if legs are heavy
  • +5 bpm for 2+ days and/or HRV well below baseline: swap quality for rest or zone 2

Cap easy days with the Zone 2 Calculator; enter accurate resting/max HR in the HR Zones Calculator.

Common Mistakes

Chasing a green Body Battery with junk volume; ignoring unilateral pain because readiness is high; comparing your HRV to a training partner; judging recovery from a single noisy night after alcohol or late food.

Practical Takeaway

Build a 2–4 week baseline on one device. Let RHR + HRV + sleep + how the warm-up feels outvote any single dashboard widget. Align flags with the overtraining signs checklist before rewriting the week.

FAQ

When should I take RHR?

Same time daily — ideally upon waking, before caffeine, same watch/wrist.

Is a lower RHR always fitter?

Lower RHR often accompanies aerobic fitness, but sudden drops with fatigue/illness context, or comparing across devices, misleads. Track change from your baseline.
