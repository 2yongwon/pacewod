Post-Run Recovery Nutrition: Protein Timing, Carbs, and the 30-Minute Window

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Wanting to “earn” a big meal later and needing glycogen/protein sooner often conflict after hard or long runs. The famous 30-minute window is most useful when another session is soon or the run was depleting — not after every easy jog.

What Actually Matters

  • Protein: ~20–40 g soon after hard/long work (toward ~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day)
  • Carbs: scale to session length/intensity — more after long/quality than after 30 min easy
  • Fluids: replace sweat losses over the next few hours

Related: long-run fueling, daily carbs, protein for runners, calories calculator.

Numeric scenario: 75 kg runner after a hard 70-min session → ~25–35 g protein + ~60–90 g carbs within ~1 hour (e.g., yogurt + fruit + bagel). After a 30-min easy jog, a normal next meal is enough if dinner is soon.

Expert Note

The recovery window is wider than Instagram suggests when daily protein and carbs are solid. Urgency rises when you train twice a day or raced long.

Decision rule: if another hard session is within 8–12 hours, prioritize carbs + protein within ~30–60 minutes. If it’s easy and dinner is soon, don’t stress the stopwatch.

What About Easy Days and Cuts?

In a calorie deficit, still prioritize protein after hard sessions; trim from lower-priority snacks, not from the recovery meal that protects the next workout. Chocolate milk, yogurt bowls, rice + eggs, or a wrap are enough — no proprietary powder required. If appetite is crushed after a race, start with liquids and follow with a real meal within a couple of hours. Track how you feel on the next quality day: flat legs after under-fueling is feedback, not mystery fatigue. Pair with sensible daily carbs from the daily carbs guide.

After very long efforts, continue carbs across the next few meals — one shake does not refill the tank. Include some sodium if you finished salty and crampy. The next easy day still needs normal meals even if you are not “hungry from training.”

Practical Takeaway

Match recovery fuel to session cost. Hard/long → deliberate protein + carbs; easy → eat on schedule.

FAQ

Do I need a fancy recovery drink?

No. Food works. Drinks help when appetite is low or you need something fast before the next session.
