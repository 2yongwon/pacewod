Post-Run Recovery Nutrition: Protein Timing, Carbs, and the 30-Minute Window

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

You finish a long run and eat 90 minutes later; your friend downs a shake in the parking lot. Did you miss a magic window? Post-run nutrition matters, but the “30-minute or waste it” marketing version oversells urgency for most recreational runners.

The Window — Myth vs Practice

Glycogen resynthesis and muscle repair start when you eat carbs and protein — sooner helps most when sessions are hard/long or another quality session is within ~8 hours. If you trained fasted, under-ate, or have doubles, prioritize a recovery meal quickly. After an easy run with normal meals around it, the next regular meal is usually enough.

Carbs, Protein, Fluids

  • Carbs: After long or hard runs, many athletes target roughly 1.0–1.2 g/kg in the first few hours (split across snack + meal), scaled to upcoming training.
  • Protein: ~20–40 g high-quality protein per feeding; daily totals often matter more than the exact minute — commonly ~1.6–2.0 g/kg/day in hard blocks (higher in a cut; see protein for runners).
  • Fluids/electrolytes: Replace sweat losses; include sodium after salty sweaters or hot sessions — not only plain water.

Simple plates that work: chocolate milk + banana; yogurt parfait with granola; rice bowl with chicken/tofu; bagel with peanut butter and a sports drink. By Run Type

  • Easy <60 min: hydrate; eat your next normal meal
  • Tempo / intervals / long run: carbs + protein within a couple of hours; sooner if another hard day is tomorrow
  • Body-comp focus: hit protein, keep carbs adequate around quality work, avoid using the run as a free-for-all

Practical Takeaway

Match recovery food to session stress. Hard/long → deliberate carbs + protein. Easy → routine meals. Daily protein and total energy beat obsessing over a 29-minute timer.

FAQ

Do I need a shake if I eat lunch in an hour?

Not required after easy runs. After a long run or intervals with lunch delayed, a small carb+protein snack bridges the gap.

Can plant-based athletes recover well?

Yes — hit total daily protein and include leucine-rich sources across meals (soy, pea blends, legumes + grains, etc.). Timing still follows session stress.
