Strides and Pickups: How to Add Speed Without Extra Fatigue

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Strides (pickups) are short, controlled accelerations that sharpen form and neuromuscular speed without the fatigue of true intervals. Done after easy runs, they keep your legs “awake” for race pace while the aerobic day stays easy.

What a Stride Is (and Is Not)

A stride is ~20–30 seconds of smooth fast running — roughly mile to 5K effort, never a sprint finish. Focus on posture, quick cadence, and relaxed arms. Full recovery walk/jog between reps so heart rate drops; if you need gasping recoveries, you turned them into intervals. Compare to structured work in the interval guide.

The Standard Prescription

  • Dose: 4–8 × 20–30 s after an easy Zone 2 run
  • Recovery: 60–90 s walk/jog between strides
  • Surface: flat, soft if possible; avoid downhill slamming
  • Frequency: 2–3 easy days per week; skip when legs are smashed

Keep the main run honest with the Zone 2 Calculator. Strides sit after cooldown starts — not mid-run as surprise “speed days.”

Form Cues Over Pace Targets

Watch pace on Garmin/COROS will look fast; ignore Training Effect badges for these. Cue tall posture, light footstrike, and cadence near or above your easy-run rate (see cadence guide). Stop a rep early if form collapses. Wrist HR lags short efforts — do not chase zone charts mid-stride.

Where They Fit in the Week

Best after easy days flanking a quality session, or the day before a workout as a primer. Do not stack strides after tempo or intervals. Before races, 4–6 strides in the warm-up wake coordination without costing glycogen.

Practical Takeaway

Add 4–8 short, smooth pickups after easy runs, recover fully, and treat them as technique — not bonus intervals. Keep easy pace via the Pace Calculator and easy run pace guide.

FAQ

Should strides feel hard?

Fast but controlled — you should finish able to talk within a minute. If you need 3+ minutes to recover, slow the next rep.

Strides vs fartlek?

Strides are short and form-focused after easy runs. Fartlek varies effort inside the run for fitness; see fartlek training.
