Sleep Tracker Accuracy: Garmin, Apple Watch, COROS, WHOOP, and Oura Compared

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Consumer trackers estimate sleep; polysomnography (PSG) measures it. Against PSG, total sleep time is usually the strongest signal; stage labels (light/deep/REM) misclassify often. Use devices for trends and behavior change — not nightly courtroom verdicts. Related: sleep and running, HRV for runners, resting HR.

Key Points

  • Trust most: time asleep / time in bed consistency week to week
  • Trust less: exact stage minutes and proprietary “sleep scores”
  • Garmin: Sleep Score + overnight HRV Status; strong for duration trends; stages are estimates
  • Apple Watch: strong for schedule/logging; stages not PSG-grade; charging can cut overnight samples on non-Ultra
  • COROS: duration/quality feeding recovery/load models; less sleep-score theater
  • WHOOP / Oura: recovery-first scoring (HRV-heavy); Oura’s ring can reduce wrist motion noise
  • Individual night error of tens of minutes on total sleep is normal versus PSG

When Scores Mislead Training

  • One low score after late caffeine or alcohol → don’t auto-skip intervals
  • High score but legs trashed → trust the warm-up
  • Comparing your Oura score to a partner’s Garmin score → meaningless across algorithms
  • Travel nights and unusual bedtimes often confuse staging more than duration

Two-week audit: log bedtime, wake time, caffeine/alcohol, and next-day easy RPE. Keep the brand that matches how you feel and that you’ll wear overnight. Switch devices mid-block only if you accept a new baseline.

If two brands disagree, pick the one you’ll wear overnight and stop cross-checking scores. Coaching decisions need a stable baseline more than a “winner” algorithm.

Practical Takeaway

Pick one wearable, wear it consistently, and manage duration. Stage charts are entertainment; 7-night sleep averages and resting HR/HRV trends are coaching data. Focus coaching changes on multi-night averages, not a single dramatic stage chart.

FAQ

Which brand is “most accurate”?

For runners, the best device is the one you wear every night with a stable baseline. Differences in stage algorithms matter less than missing nights.

Should I buy a ring and a watch?

Usually no. Dual devices create conflicting scores. One consistent overnight signal plus how you feel on the run is enough.
