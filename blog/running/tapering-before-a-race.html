Tapering Before a Race: Volume, Intensity, and Why Legs Feel Heavy

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

After a long build, “run less” feels like fitness leaking away. Adding sharpeners to quiet the doubt is how athletes arrive tired. Tapering cuts fatigue while keeping race-ready neuromuscular sharpness — volume down, intensity briefly maintained.

How Long and How Much

  • 5K / 10K: often ~7–10 days of reduced volume
  • Half marathon: commonly ~10–14 days
  • Marathon: often ~2–3 weeks, progressive

Typical pattern: cut weekly volume ~40–60% (sometimes more into race week) while keeping some short race-pace or faster strides/reps. Cardinal rule: maintain intensity, cut duration and easy-mile frequency. Eliminating all quality work often leaves you flat; stacking extra quality leaves you heavy. Sample half-marathon idea: two weeks out cut ~30–40%, race week cut ~50–60%, with one short goal-pace touch mid-week.

Heavy Legs and Glycogen

Sluggish easy jogs during taper are normal — less training stimulus, shifting glycogen and water, anxious brain. Do not “test” with a surprise threshold. For events longer than ~60–75 minutes, reduced load plus ~8–10 g carbs/kg/day in the final ~3 days can raise glycogen stores ~10–20% above baseline. Loading takes 48–72 hours; one pasta dinner is not enough. For 5K/10K, normal carb-forward meals usually suffice.

Common Taper Mistakes

  • Adding workouts to chase freshness
  • Cutting all intensity and feeling flat
  • Making race week a crash diet or a buffet experiment
  • New shoes, new gels, hard course efforts

Day-by-day logistics live in Race Week Preparation.

Connect Taper to Race Week

The last seven days are logistics: sleep, familiar carbs, packed gear, and no fitness tests. If legs feel wooden on a shakeout, keep it short — freshness shows up on the start line, not in Tuesday’s easy jog. Print splits early so anxiety does not invent a faster goal mid-taper.

Practical Takeaway

Protect freshness: fewer miles, short race-pace touches, practiced carbs for longer events, early sleep. Trust the work already done. Use the Pace Chart for goal splits so taper anxiety does not rewrite your race plan.

FAQ

Will I lose fitness if I cut volume 50%?

Not in a proper 1–3 week taper. You shed fatigue faster than fitness. Short intensity maintains race-pace feel.

Do I need a full carb load for a 5K?

Usually no. Normal carb-forward meals are enough. Save aggressive loading for longer races where glycogen is limiting.
