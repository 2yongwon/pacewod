Return to Running After a Break: 4-Week Progression Without Reinjury

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Fitness returns faster than tissue tolerance. VO2 may drop only ~5–10% after 2–4 weeks off, but tendons and bone need longer to handle impact again. Restart from what tissues tolerate now — often 15–25% of prior peak — not last month’s mileage.

Clearance Before Week 1

For injury breaks: pain-free walking 30+ minutes, stairs, and (if relevant) single-leg hop with no next-day flare; get medical clearance for stress injuries/surgery. Focal bone pain needs evaluation first. Breaks under 2 weeks need less conservatism; 4+ weeks need the full progression; 8+ weeks may need 6–8 weeks rebuild.

4-Week Progression

  • Week 1: 3 walk-run sessions (e.g., 1 min run / 1–2 min walk × 8–12). Flat soft surface. Cap HR below ~70% max or top of zone 2 (HR Zones Calculator). Stop if pain >2/10 during or >3/10 next morning.
  • Week 2: Longer run segments (3–8 min) with short walks; ~35–50 min total running. Cap ~75% max HR. Do not exceed ~150% of week-1 running minutes.
  • Week 3: Continuous easy 20–30 min, 3–4 sessions (~65–90 min total). Expect easy pace 60–90 sec/km slower than before. Cap ~75–80% max HR / top of zone 2 (Zone 2 Calculator).
  • Week 4: ~90–115 min total across 4 easy sessions; apply the 10% rule to this new baseline — not pre-break volume.

When to Add Quality

No intervals/tempos in the 4-week block. Reintroduce only after: 4+ pain-free easy weeks, volume ≥70% of prior, RHR within 2 bpm of baseline for 7+ days, and longest easy run 60–75 minutes without next-day pain. Then: strides → fartlek → short tempo → intervals, one new stimulus per week, without raising volume the same week.

Red Flags

Worsening pain during a run, next-morning pain >3/10 or gait change, swelling/focal bone tenderness, RHR +5 bpm for 3+ days, or return of the original injury pain — drop back a full week, not one session.

Practical Takeaway

Walk-run first, cap HR, grow volume ~10%/week from the new baseline. Lungs will feel ready before tendons are — trust the table. Tools: Pace Calculator, shin splints guide.

FAQ

How long a break needs this plan?

≤2 weeks: one reduced easy week is often enough. 3–4 weeks: full 4-week progression. 6+ weeks or injury: full plan, expect 8–16 weeks to prior volume.

It feels too easy — can I skip ahead?

No. Cardiovascular ease is the trap. Advance only after a clean week (pain ≤2/10, stable RHR).
