Garmin vs COROS Heart Rate Accuracy

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Wrist optical HR from Garmin and COROS is usually fine for steady easy runs. It is not interchangeable with a chest strap during intervals, cold starts, or fast transitions. The useful question: when is wrist data decision-grade for today’s workout?

Chest Strap Still Wins

A snug, moistened ECG strap typically tracks within ~1–3 bpm of reference in steady aerobic work. Wrist LEDs infer pulse from blood volume — motion, perfusion, temperature, and fit add noise. Published tests often show ~3–5% MAPE for wrist optical in running (tighter easy, wider in intervals) vs ~1–2% for straps. At 170 bpm, 5% error is an 8–9 beat swing — enough to mis-tag a zone. Set zones with the HR Zones Calculator.

Garmin Wrist HR

Elevate sensors on recent Forerunner/Fenix lines are well-tested. Strong on steady easy/long runs after a 5–10 minute warm-up with a snug fit (two finger-widths above the wrist bone). Weaker in cold (often 10–20 bpm low early), short intervals (lag/overshoot), sprints, and mixed modality CrossFit with grip changes.

COROS Wrist HR

COROS optical performs similarly on steady aerobic work when fit is good. Same failure modes: cold vasoconstriction, interval lag, cadence/arm-swing artifact. Neither brand “wins” universally in independent tests — unit fit, skin, and workout type dominate brand marketing charts.

When to Trust Wrist vs Strap

  • Wrist OK: zone 2, long easy runs, warm weather, steady cadence — validate with the Zone 2 Calculator
  • Use strap: intervals, tempo, threshold tests, cold starts, treadmill HR-capped sessions
  • Always cross-check: RPE and pace when HR looks impossible

Training load / VO2 estimates on both platforms inherit HR errors — garbage HR in, garbage status out (Garmin Training Status).

Practical Takeaway

Hierarchy: chest strap > wrist on steady runs > wrist during intervals/cold. Pick either brand for easy volume; wear a strap when zones decide the workout. Related: Apple Watch vs Garmin, chest strap guide.

FAQ

Which is more accurate, Garmin or COROS?

For steady easy running, both are typically close enough. Differences show up most in intervals and cold — use a strap rather than hunting a “winner.”

Why does my watch read low for the first mile in winter?

Cold reduces wrist blood flow; optical sensors under-read until you warm up. Start by feel or use a chest strap.
