RPE vs Heart Rate Training

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Heart rate measures cardiac response. RPE measures perceived effort. Neither is perfect alone — the skill is knowing which signal to trust when they conflict.

What Each Measures

HR: objective, lagging (especially wrist optical), altered by heat, caffeine, dehydration, stress, and cardiac drift (drift & decoupling). RPE: immediate, portable, but skewed by mood, group pace, and fitness naivety. Calibrate RPE against HR over a few weeks using the HR Zones Calculator and Zone 2 Calculator.

Easy Days

  • Trust HR in heat, on hills, when “it feels easy” but HR is Zone 3, or when ego/group pace pulls hard.
  • Trust RPE / talk test when wrist HR is glitchy, very cold, or clearly lagging; still keep conversational speech.
  • Best practice: both must pass — conversational and under Zone 2 ceiling.

Hard Days

  • Trust pace + RPE for short intervals (HR lags reps). Use the Pace Calculator for targets.
  • Trust HR for longer threshold/tempo blocks once HR stabilizes, or when heat makes race-pace reckless.
  • Example: 5×1000m at threshold — hit pace/RPE each rep; ignore mid-rep HR spikes from lag.

Mixed-Signal Decision Tree

  1. Is the session easy or hard by plan?
  2. Easy + high HR → slow down (conditions or gray-zone creep).
  3. Easy + low HR but hard feel → cut short or rest (fatigue/illness).
  4. Hard + HR low but pace/RPE on target → trust pace/RPE (lag or strong day).
  5. Hard + HR sky-high early → extend warm-up or adjust for heat; don’t force the full set.

Calibrating Over a Block

For 2–3 weeks, log RPE (1–10) next to average HR on easy runs and one tempo. You want easy = RPE 2–3 at Zone 2 HR, tempo ≈ RPE 6–7 near LT HR. If RPE 3 always lands at Zone 3 HR, your “easy” feeling is biased high — trust the strap until feel recalibrates.

Practical Takeaway

Use HR as a governor on easy days and RPE/pace as the primary driver on short hard work. Recalibrate monthly: if RPE 3 always lands at the top of Zone 2, your scale is working.

FAQ

Why does HR say Zone 3 when the run feels easy?

Heat, residual fatigue, dehydration, or a fitness jump that left zones stale. Slow to the HR cap anyway on easy days.
