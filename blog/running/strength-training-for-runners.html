Strength Training for Runners: Exercises, Frequency, and Leg Day Scheduling

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Each footstrike loads roughly 2–3× body weight. Strength work builds hip stability, single-leg control, and tendon resilience that mileage alone does not — insurance for the thousands of ground contacts in a block, plus better running economy.

Best Exercises

Prioritize single-leg and posterior-chain work:

  • Primary: Bulgarian split squats (3×8–10/leg), Romanian deadlifts (3×8–12), step-ups (3×10/leg), calf raises straight + bent knee (3×15–20)
  • Support: goblet squats, single-leg RDLs, glute bridges/hip thrusts
  • Core: Pallof press, dead bugs/bird dogs, side planks (anti-rotation/anti-extension)
  • Upper: one push + one pull for posture maintenance

After 6–8 weeks of consistent lifting, optional low-volume plyos (e.g., 3–4×5 box jumps) once weekly — not in peak mileage weeks or within 48 hours of a long run.

Frequency and Session Shape

Most runners thrive on two 30–40 minute sessions/week. Warm-up 5 min → 2–3 lower compounds + core → optional upper. Mix heavier (4–6 reps, RPE 7–8) and moderate (8–12, RPE 6–7). Leave 1–2 reps in reserve. Beginners: start with one session; add a second after ~3 weeks.

Schedule Around Key Runs

Never heavy legs the day before a long run or intervals. Best: run easy first, lift after; or lift with 48+ hours before the next quality session. Sample 45 mpw week: Mon easy + heavy lower; Tue intervals; Thu tempo; Sat moderate strength; Sun long run. Beginners: one mid-week full-body session far from the long run.

Periodize With the Season

  • Base: highest strength volume, progressive load
  • Build: maintain; protect quality runs
  • Taper: one short/light session — do not go to zero for three weeks

CrossFit athletes: random AMRAPs do not replace structured single-leg work; keep hard WODs 48+ hours from long runs. Check easy pace with the Pace Calculator — if easy pace slows ~30 sec after lifting, you lifted too hard or too close.

Practical Takeaway

Two focused sessions, single-leg + posterior chain, never before key runs. Progress load in base, maintain in build, taper gym volume like miles. See the running recovery guide.

FAQ

Will lifting make me slower?

Not with moderate compound sessions at maintenance calories. Bodybuilding volume plus surplus can add mass; two short runner-focused sessions usually improve economy and durability.

Run before or after lifting?

Run first when the run is the priority. Never stack hard intervals and heavy legs the same day.
