Best Heart Rate for Fat Burn

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

The “fat-burning zone” on gym posters is a half-truth. Fat oxidation rate often peaks near Zone 2, but fat loss is driven by weekly energy balance — not maximizing the percentage of calories from fat in one jog.

What the Fat-Burn Zone Gets Right (and Wrong)

At easy aerobic effort (~60–70% max HR / Zone 2), a higher share of fuel comes from fat. At harder efforts, carb share rises and total calorie burn per minute rises too. For weight loss, total weekly expenditure and diet usually matter more than staying in the peak fat-oxidation band every run.

Set zones with the Zone 2 Calculator and HR Zones Calculator. Estimate session burn with the Running Calories Calculator.

Zone 2’s Real Job

  • Builds aerobic base so you can handle more weekly volume
  • Improves fat oxidation capacity for endurance
  • Keeps recovery cost low so you can add some harder work

Zone 2 alone does not guarantee fat loss if intake rises with appetite or if weekly load is tiny.

Should You Still Do Hard Sessions?

Yes, in moderation. Intervals raise calorie burn and fitness (including VO2-related capacity — see VO2 Max Calculator), which supports higher sustainable training volume. A practical fat-loss week: mostly Zone 2 volume + 1 quality session + strength, with a modest calorie deficit you can recover on.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating 220−age formulas as exact science
  • Running “easy” days in Zone 3 (gray zone) — more fatigue, less volume
  • Chasing only peak fat % instead of weekly volume + nutrition
  • Trusting watch zone charts without fixing max HR / LTHR

Garmin/COROS zone labels and calorie estimates are approximate; optical HR errors compound calorie math. Prefer chest strap for zone discipline; use pace from the Pace Calculator as a secondary check.

Practical Takeaway

Best HR for training that supports fat loss: honest Zone 2 for most runs, plus limited hard work. Best “zone” for fat loss on the scale: consistent deficit you can sleep and train through. Easy aerobic work is a tool for volume and recovery — not a magic fat furnace.

FAQ

Is low HR better than hard intervals for fat loss?

Low HR supports more weekly minutes. Hard intervals help fitness and burn. Mix both; don’t live only at either extreme.
