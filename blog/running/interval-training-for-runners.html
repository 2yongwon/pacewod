Interval Training for Runners

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Intervals break hard work into repeats with recovery so you can spend more time near target pace than in a single continuous effort. Anchor paces first, then pick the rep length that matches the stimulus you want.

Anchor Paces

Convert a recent race with the Pace Calculator and Race Predictor. Typical anchors: 5K pace (VO2), 10K pace (high aerobic), threshold/tempo, and mile/3K for shorter speed.

Core Sessions

  • 400s: 8–10 × 400m @ ~3K–5K, 200m jog — speed endurance / VO2
  • 800s: 5–6 × 800m @ 5K with equal jog — classic VO2
  • Threshold 800s: 6 × 800m @ 10K with short recovery — aerobic strength
  • Mile repeats: 3–5 × 1 mile @ threshold or controlled 5K, full recoveries as needed
  • Yasso 800s: marathon benchmark tool — useful check, not magic prediction

Recovery and Structure

  • VO2 reps: recoveries often ~equal to work (or jog 200–400m)
  • Threshold reps: shorter recoveries (45–90 s) to keep LT stress
  • Always: warm-up 15–20 min + strides; cool-down easy
  • HR lags short reps — pace/RPE lead; wrist HR is especially noisy (HR accuracy)

Weekly Placement

One interval day for most runners; advanced may add a second quality day (tempo). Surround with easy Zone 2. Progress one variable at a time: reps → pace → shorter recovery.

Scaling and Progression

New to intervals? Start with 4–5 × 800m or 6–8 × 400m at controlled 5K effort — finish the set able to do one more. Next, add a rep, then nudge pace, then shorten recovery. Miss more than one rep’s target by a large margin? End the set; quality over completionism.

Place intervals mid-week or when you’re freshest. Long-run day stays mostly easy.

FAQ

Heart rate or pace for intervals?

Pace and RPE for reps under ~5 minutes. Use HR for longer threshold intervals once it stabilizes, and on hot days as a safety governor.

Practical Takeaway

Intervals work when targets are honest and easy days stay easy. Miss the pace late in the set? Stop or extend recovery — broken reps beat junk volume at the wrong intensity.
