Interval Training for Runners: 400m, 800m, and Mile Repeats With Real Paces

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Intervals raise speed and VO2 — until ego turns every rep into a time trial. The conflict is hard enough to adapt vs controllable enough to finish the set with even splits. Anchor paces from a recent race with the Pace Calculator and Race Predictor; treat wrist HR as laggy on short reps (see HR accuracy).

Match Rep Distance to the Job

  • 400m: speed/VO2 touch — ~3K–5K effort, fuller recoveries
  • 800m: classic VO2 — near 3K–5K pace, jog recoveries ~equal time or slightly less
  • Mile repeats: threshold–VO2 bridge — closer to 10K–threshold effort, longer recoveries

Write the session before you lace up: rep count, target split, recovery mode. Guessing mid-set usually means the first rep is a sprint and the last reps are survival.

Numeric scenario: Recent 5K in 25:00 → ~5:00/km. A workable VO2 session: 6 × 800m at ~4:50–4:55/km (≈3:52–3:56 per 800) with 2:30–3:00 jog. If rep 1 is 3:40 and rep 5 is 4:10, you opened too hot — next time start 4–6 sec/800 slower and hold ±3 sec across the set.

Expert Note

Equal or slightly negative splits across the set beat a heroic first rep. If form collapses (shoulders hike, cadence tanks), the stimulus becomes injury risk, not VO2 work. Warm up 10–15 minutes plus a few strides before rep one; cool down 10 minutes easy.

Decision rule: cut the session short or slow the remaining reps if two consecutive reps miss target by >5 sec/400 (or equivalent) and RPE is already 9/10 with incomplete recovery.

Recovery and Weekly Placement

Jog recoveries keep blood moving; standing recoveries suit pure speed. Most runners need only one hard interval day per week (plus optional lighter quality). Easy days must stay easy the day after — if they don’t, the interval dose was too large. Skip stacking intervals the day before a long run.

Practical Takeaway

Pick the rep length for the stimulus, set pace from race fitness, and judge success by even splits — not by whether the first 400 felt impressive on Strava.

FAQ

Should I use heart rate for intervals?

Pace and RPE lead on reps under ~3 minutes. HR is better for checking that easy days recover afterward.
