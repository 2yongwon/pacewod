Interval Training for Runners: 400m, 800m, and Mile Repeats With Real Paces

Updated August 2026 · 13 min read

The workout says "6 × 800m at 5K pace." Your recent 5K is 22:30 — that's 4:30/km, so each 800m should take 3:36. Rep 1 feels fine at 3:34. Rep 4 is 3:42 and your heart rate peaks during the jog recovery. You are not unfit; you miscalibrated recovery ratio, started too fast, or used stale race fitness from six months ago. Intervals fail when pace targets float untethered from current ability.

Interval training works when rep distance, pace, recovery, and rep count match a physiological target — VO2 max, lactate threshold, or race-specific endurance — and when you can hit the last rep within 2–3 seconds of the first. Random track sessions produce sweat, not adaptation.

This article prescribes 400m, 800m, and mile (1609m) repeat workouts with real pace tables anchored to current 5K and 10K fitness, explains recovery ratios that preserve rep quality, and shows how to progress sessions across a training block without turning Tuesday into a weekly bonk.

Anchor Paces Before You Hit the Track

All repeat paces derive from recent race or time trial performance — ideally within the last 6–8 weeks. Use:

  • 5K race time — primary anchor for 400m and 800m VO2 sessions
  • 10K race time — primary anchor for mile repeats and threshold-leaning work
  • 3K time trial — if no recent 5K, multiply by ~1.05–1.08 for 5K equivalent

Convert race times to training paces with the Pace Calculator or Race Predictor. Below, example splits assume three reference runners — adjust proportionally to your times:

  • Runner A: 5K 20:00 (4:00/km) · 10K 41:30 (4:09/km)
  • Runner B: 5K 25:00 (5:00/km) · 10K 52:00 (5:12/km)
  • Runner C: 5K 30:00 (6:00/km) · 10K 62:30 (6:15/km)

Pace zones for intervals (approximate):

  • VO2 max (3K–5K pace feel): 5K pace to 3–5 sec/km faster than 5K pace
  • Threshold (10K–15K pace feel): 10K pace to 5K pace + 5–10 sec/km
  • Race-specific: goal 5K or 10K pace for longer reps
Rep quality rule

Stop or extend recovery when the last rep is more than 3% slower than the first (e.g., 3:36 first 800m → 3:43 last is acceptable; 3:50 is not). Consistent splits beat heroic rep 1. If rep 3 collapses, you started too fast, recovery is too short, or anchor pace is stale — fix the variable, do not grind through junk reps.

400m Repeats: Speed Endurance and VO2

400m reps train neuromuscular speed endurance and high-end aerobic power. Total hard distance per session typically 3200–4800m (8–12 × 400m). Recovery is relatively generous because rep intensity is high.

Classic VO2 set: 8–10 × 400m

Target: 5K pace to 2–4 sec/km faster. Recovery: 200m jog or 90 sec walk/jog (1:1 to 1:1.5 work:rest ratio).

Progression: start with 6 × 400m in week 1, add 2 reps every other week, cap at 10–12. Alternate with 800m sessions — do not run hard 400s and hard 800s in the same week unless advanced and well recovered.

Float 400s: 12 × 400m at 5K pace

Target: exact 5K pace. Recovery: 100m jog (30–45 sec) — shorter rest, moderate intensity. Builds lactate clearance and race rhythm. Runner B example: 12 × 2:00 at 5:00/km with 100m jogs. Last 4 reps should feel like 5K race effort — RPE 8/10.

Downward ladder: 400–800–400

Format: 2–3 sets of (400m fast, 400m jog, 800m at 5K pace, 400m jog, 400m fast). Fast 400s at 3K effort (~5 sec/km faster than 5K). Teaches pace change without full recovery between distances. Session total: moderate volume, high intensity spikes.

800m Repeats: The Core VO2 Workout

800m reps sit at the center of distance running intervals — long enough to stress VO2 max, short enough to accumulate 4–6 km of quality work. Standard recovery: 400m jog or 2–3 min between reps (roughly 1:1 work:rest duration).

Standard VO2: 5–6 × 800m

Target: 5K pace (some coaches use 5K pace + 2 sec/km for longer sets). Recovery: 400m jog (~2:00–2:30).

Example session (Runner B): 2 km warm-up, drills, 5 × 800m in 4:00–4:02 with 400m jog recovery, 1.5 km cool-down. Success metric: rep 5 within 2 sec of rep 1.

Extended VO2: 4 × 800m at 3K effort

Target: 3–5 sec/km faster than 5K pace. Fewer reps, higher intensity. Runner A: 4 × 2:58–3:02. Recovery: 3 min jog. Use during peak 5K phase, 3–4 weeks before goal race, once every 7–10 days.

Threshold-leaning: 6 × 800m at 10K pace

Target: 10K race pace — roughly 5K pace + 8–12 sec/km. Lower RPE (7/10), shorter recovery needs. Runner B: 6 × 4:10–4:14 with 200m jog. Bridges tempo and VO2 work; useful early build phase before pure 5K-pace 800s.

Yasso 800s (marathon benchmark)

Popularized as marathon predictor: run 800m reps in minutes:seconds matching marathon goal hours:minutes (e.g., 3:30 marathon → 3:30 per 800m). Reality: useful VO2 session for marathoners, weak predictor for non-elites. Marathon 3:45 runner doing 10 × 3:45 800s with equal rest is a hard workout — treat as VO2, not prophecy.

Mile Repeats: Threshold and Race Endurance

Mile (1609m) repeats target lactate threshold, 10K race fitness, and muscular endurance. Recovery: 400m–800m jog or 3–4 min (often 1:0.5 to 1:0.75 work:rest ratio because rep duration is longer). Total hard volume: 4–8 km.

Threshold miles: 4–5 × 1 mile

Target: 10K pace to 10K pace + 5 sec/km. Primary build-phase workout for 10K and half marathoners.

5K-pace miles: 3 × 1 mile

Target: 5K race pace. High load — use sparingly 4–6 weeks before 5K peak. Runner A: 3 × 6:26 (4:00/km) with 4 min jog. Marathoners rarely need 5K-pace miles; half marathoners use 2–3 reps as race-sharpening.

1200m–mile broken sets

Format: 3 × (1200m at 10K pace, 400m jog, 400m at 5K pace). Combines threshold volume with speed kick. Total quality: 3 × 1600m equivalent. Useful when full miles at 10K pace feel too monolithic.

Cutdown miles: 3 × mile descending pace

Rep 1 at 10K pace + 5 sec/km, rep 2 at 10K pace, rep 3 at 10K pace − 5 sec/km or 5K pace. Teaches finishing strong. Advanced only; 3 min recovery between reps.

Recovery Ratios That Preserve Rep Quality

Recovery is part of the prescription, not filler:

  • 400m VO2: 1:1 to 1:1.5 (400m run → 400–600m jog or 90–120 sec)
  • 800m VO2: 1:1 duration (3:40 rep → 3:00–3:40 jog)
  • 800m threshold: 1:0.5 (200–400m jog sufficient)
  • Mile threshold: 1:0.5 to 1:0.75 (2.5–4 min jog)
  • Mile at 5K pace: 1:1 or longer (4–5 min jog)

Standing rest is valid for 400m speed reps; jogging recovery maintains HR elevation for VO2 sessions but must be easy — truly easy jog, not stride. If recovery jog HR stays above 120 bpm entering next rep on 800s, extend rest 30 sec.

Session Structure: Warm-Up Through Cool-Down

  1. 15–20 min easy jog — progressive to light aerobic
  2. Dynamic drills — leg swings, A-skips, 4 × 80m strides building to rep pace feel
  3. Rep set — start rep 1 at target, not faster; bank speed for rep 6
  4. 10–15 min easy cool-down — non-negotiable for lactate clearance

Track vs road: track gives exact splits; road loops need measured segments. Treadmill intervals work for 400m–800m if outdoor track unavailable — set speed to target pace, 1% incline optional to match outdoor metabolic cost.

Weekly Placement and Progression

One interval session per week suffices for most 5K–half marathon plans. Marathoners may use mile repeats at half marathon pace every 10–14 days instead of pure VO2 800s during base. Sample 4-week VO2 block (Runner B, 25:00 5K):

  • Week 1: 5 × 800m at 4:02, 400m jog
  • Week 2: 8 × 400m at 1:58, 200m jog
  • Week 3: 6 × 800m at 4:00, 400m jog
  • Week 4: 4 × mile at 8:22 (10K pace), 400m jog — threshold bridge

Do not stack intervals day before long run. Prefer 48+ hours after previous hard session. Easy days after intervals must be truly easy — HR cap or shuffle — or the next quality session fails.

Heart Rate and RPE on Intervals

HR lags on 400m and 800m reps — do not use wrist HR to judge rep 1 effort. Use pace and RPE. By rep 3–4 on 800s, HR approaches 90–95% max if recovery was adequate. Mile reps at threshold show HR near LTHR after 2–3 minutes within each rep. See heart rate accuracy guide for sensor choice on interval days — chest strap or pace-primary.

Scaling to Your Current Fitness

Linear scaling from the tables: divide your 5K time by 12.5 for approximate 400m VO2 target; divide 5K time by 6.25 for 800m at 5K pace; divide 10K time by 6.2 for mile at 10K pace. Example: 5K in 23:45 (5:42/km) → 800m at 5K pace ≈ 4:34–4:36. Verify with the Pace Calculator before the session.

If no recent race, run a 20-min time trial on flat terrain — average pace approximates lactate threshold; 5K equivalent ≈ threshold pace minus 10–15 sec/km for trained runners.

Common Interval Mistakes

Rep 1 heroics: 5 sec too fast on rep 1 costs 15 sec on rep 5. Even splits win.

Stale pace anchors: training paces from a PR 8 months ago produce blow-ups. Re-test every 6–8 weeks.

Short recovery for ego: cutting rest turns VO2 work into anaerobic survival — extend jog rest until splits hold.

Too many reps: 10 × 800m at true 5K pace is 8 km at VO2 intensity — elite territory. Most runners stop at 5–6.

Ignoring context: heat and wind adjust targets ±3–5 sec/km; same effort, not same clock split.

Final Takeaway

400m repeats at 5K pace to slightly faster build VO2 with 8–10 reps and generous jog recovery. 800m repeats at 5K pace are the standard VO2 session — 5–6 reps with 400m jogs, last rep matching first. Mile repeats at 10K pace develop threshold for 10K and half marathon racing — 4–5 reps with 3–4 min recovery.

Anchor every split to current 5K or 10K fitness, progress rep count before pace, and abort when splits degrade beyond 3%. Intervals are math tied to physiology — when the paces are honest, the adaptation follows.

FAQ

How many 800m repeats should I do?

Most runners perform 5–6 × 800m at 5K pace for a standard VO2 max session — 4.0–4.8 km of hard running plus warm-up and cool-down. Beginners may start with 4 × 800m and add one rep every other week. Elite 5K specialists occasionally reach 8 × 800m, but diminishing returns and recovery cost rise sharply beyond 6 reps at true VO2 intensity.

Should 400m repeats be faster than 5K pace?

Classic VO2 400m reps target 5K pace to 2–4 seconds per km faster — roughly 3K effort feel. Float 400s at exact 5K pace with short recovery are slightly easier per rep but harder to accumulate due to minimal rest. Alternate formats weekly rather than running all-out 400s every session.

What pace should mile repeats be for a half marathon goal?

Run mile repeats at 10K race pace to half marathon pace depending on phase: 10K pace (RPE 7–8/10) during build for lactate threshold; half marathon pace (RPE 6–7/10) during early base or marathon-specific blocks. A 1:45 half marathon runner (5:00/km) might run 4–5 × mile at 4:55–5:05 with 3 min jog recovery.

Can I do intervals without a track?

Yes. Use a measured flat loop, treadmill, or GPS segment with consistent distance markers. Road intervals at the same pace targets work identically if terrain is flat and wind is moderate. The track advantage is exact splits and no traffic — not a physiological requirement.
