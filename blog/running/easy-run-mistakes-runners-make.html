Easy Run Mistakes Runners Make

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Easy runs fail when they become moderate. The fix is not more motivation — it is anchors (HR, talk test, intent) and context (heat, hills, sleep). Below are the mistakes that quietly flatten training.

What “Easy” Means

Conversational Zone 2 effort you could continue for another hour. Not “a bit slower than tempo.” Set ranges with the Zone 2 Calculator and HR Zones Calculator; see Easy Run Pace Guide.

Mistakes That Matter

  • Feel without anchors: fatigue and fitness skew RPE. Pair feel with HR ceiling.
  • Strava / mileage chasing: segment hunting turns recovery into tempo. Mute segments on easy days.
  • Faster partners: group pace ≠ your Zone 2. Run alone or state the cap upfront.
  • Average pace worship: hills and heat inflate HR at “easy” pace. Cap HR, not pace.
  • Wrist HR reading low: optical can under-read at easy effort — you think Zone 1 while you’re Zone 2–3. Validate with a chest strap; see Garmin vs COROS HR accuracy.
  • Every run as “training”: some runs exist only to recover. Not every session needs a Training Effect badge.
  • Ignoring context: heat, hills, poor sleep (sleep & performance) raise cost — slow down.
  • Confusing easy with short: easy can be long; short hard is not easy.

How You Know You’re Doing It

  • Easy runs finish in Zone 3 average HR
  • Hard days feel flat; resting HR creeps up
  • Talk test fails on “recovery” days
  • Pace-at-fixed-HR stagnates despite high weekly miles

The 80/20 Fix

Keep ~80% of weekly time truly easy (Zone 2), ~20% hard. Example at ~40 miles: one interval, one tempo/threshold, rest easy + long. If easy days drift gray, you no longer have polarized training — you have chronic moderate. Details: Zone 2 vs Zone 3, Zone 2 Guide.

Quick Weekly Audit

After seven days, check: What % of running time was truly Zone 2? If hard + moderate exceeds ~25–30% without a race week reason, easy-run mistakes are in control. Reset with two strict HR-capped weeks before adding quality back.

Practical Takeaway

Decide the purpose before the run. Cap HR, walk hills if needed, and treat Strava pace as noise. Easy discipline is what makes hard days productive.
