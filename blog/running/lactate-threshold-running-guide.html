Lactate Threshold Running Guide

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Lactate threshold (LT) is the highest intensity you can sustain while clearing lactate roughly as fast as you produce it — roughly 30–60 minutes of hard effort for trained runners. It anchors tempo work and, often, better Zone 2 boundaries than max HR.

LT Heart Rate vs LT Pace

Train with both. Pace is the primary target on cool, flat days; HR catches heat and fatigue when the same pace becomes too costly. Zones from LTHR usually beat 220−age defaults — see LTHR vs Max HR and the HR Zones Calculator.

Field Test Protocols

  • 30-min TT: full warm-up, then 30 min hardest sustainable effort; LTHR ≈ average HR of last 20 minutes; LT pace ≈ average pace of full 30.
  • 20-min Friel-style: hard 20 min after warm-up; use average HR as LTHR estimate (often slightly higher than true LT — know the model you apply).

Example: last-20 HR 168 bpm, 30-min pace 4:20/km → set tempo near that pace/HR; build Zone 2 as ~85–89% of 168 if using Friel running zones. Cross-check race fitness with the Race Predictor.

Watch Estimates vs Field Test

Garmin auto-LT and COROS threshold estimates are convenient drafts. They can lag after fitness jumps or skew on heat/hills. Prefer a controlled field test when watch LT and recent race/tempo effort disagree by more than a few bpm or several sec/km. Details in the Tempo Run Guide.

Training Around LT

  • Continuous tempo: 20–40 min near LT
  • Cruise intervals: e.g. 3–5 × 8–10 min at LT with short jog recoveries
  • Keep most other mileage easy so LT sessions stay sharp

Common LT Mistakes

  • Sandbagging or blasting the field test (both warp LTHR)
  • Copying bike LT HR into running zones
  • Doing threshold work when easy days were secretly Zone 3 all week
  • Updating Garmin LT from one hot hilly run and shifting every zone overnight

Practical Takeaway

Test LT every 6–8 weeks in a build, enter LTHR into your watch, and use pace + HR together. Threshold is a trainable speed limit — protect it with easy volume, don’t bury it in gray-zone junk.

FAQ

Is LT the same as cycling FTP?

Same idea, different sport. Do not copy bike FTP watts or HR straight into running zones.
