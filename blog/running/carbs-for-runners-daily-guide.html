Daily Carbs for Runners: How Much Outside of Race Day

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Race-day gels get the headlines; daily carbs decide whether workouts feel sharp or flat. Match carbohydrate intake to training load — not to a fixed “low carb” identity — and quality sessions stop drifting.

Targets by Training Load (g/kg)

Use body weight in kilograms. Rough daily ranges used in sports nutrition practice:

  • Easy / low volume: ~3–5 g/kg
  • Moderate (most training weeks): ~5–7 g/kg
  • Hard / high volume or doubles: ~6–10 g/kg
  • Extreme load / race week fueling: toward the high end, timed around sessions

Example: 70 kg runner on a moderate week ≈ 350–490 g carbs/day. Scale with the Calories Calculator as a rough energy check, not gospel.

Easy Days vs Hard Days

Periodize carbs with the calendar. Easy Zone 2 days can sit lower in the range; interval/tempo and long-run days deserve more, especially in the meal before and after. Keep protein steady; swing carbs with intensity. See post-run recovery nutrition for the after-window.

For runs >90 minutes, in-session fueling matters separately — follow long-run fueling (often ~30–60+ g carbs/hour).

Carbs in a Calorie Deficit

Cutting weight? Prefer a modest deficit and protect carbs around quality sessions rather than cutting them first. Very low daily carbs plus hard intervals is a common recipe for gray-zone easy runs and stalled pace. Details: calorie deficit for runners and protein while cutting.

Watch and App Noise

Garmin/COROS calorie burn is often optimistic. Do not match every estimated calorie with food from the watch alone. Use training feel, body-weight trend, and workout quality. If easy pace at fixed HR worsens while “eating to the watch,” you are likely underfueled.

Practical Takeaway

Eat ~3–7+ g/kg carbs by load, push higher on hard and long days, and do not starve quality sessions during a cut. Link daily intake with long-run fueling and race week plans (race-day nutrition).

FAQ

Do I need carbs before every easy run?

Short easy runs can be fine with normal meals. Longer or morning-fasted hard efforts usually need planned carbs.

Are low-carb diets good for marathon training?

Most runners perform better with adequate carbs on hard days. Experiment carefully; do not debut low-carb in peak weeks.
