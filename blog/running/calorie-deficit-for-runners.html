Calorie Deficit for Runners: How Large, How Long, and When It Kills Performance

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Runners track paces to the second, then guess at deficits. The useful middle is a quantified, moderate cut you can hold long enough to change composition without dismantling aerobic work.

The 300–500 kcal Guideline

Most runners do well with roughly 300–500 kcal/day below maintenance (sometimes ~0.25–0.5% body weight per week). Larger cuts speed the scale and often kill workouts by week three. Estimate TDEE from a few weeks of intake at stable weight, or a calculator plus reality checks — not from eating back every Garmin calorie.

Example: a 70 kg runner might hold ~2,400–2,600 kcal maintenance depending on volume, then eat ~2,000–2,200 in a cut while protecting long-run carbs. Use the Running Calories Calculator for session ranges; apply a haircut to watch totals (accuracy guide).

Protein and Duration

Hold protein near 1.6–2.2 g/kg while cutting (e.g., 70 kg → ~110–155 g/day). Keep carbs around quality sessions. Plan deficit blocks of several weeks with diet breaks if training quality or mood crashes. Strength 1–2×/week helps retain muscle. See protein for runners.

When the Deficit Kills Performance

  • Easy pace slows at the same HR, or HR rises at the same pace
  • Resting HR up ~5–10 bpm for several mornings / HRV trending down
  • Sleep, mood, or injury niggles stack up
  • Quality sessions consistently miss targets

Response: raise calories toward maintenance (especially carbs), keep protein, and pause the cut — don’t add more intervals to “earn” food.

Watch Calories and the Eat-Back Trap

If Garmin reports 900 kcal for a run the MET estimate puts near 650, eating the full 900 can erase a planned 400 kcal deficit in one snack. Prefer midpoint or lower-bound estimates for cuts, and keep the deficit in the meal plan — not renegotiated after every workout screenshot.

Practical Takeaway

Moderate deficit, high protein, honest calorie math, protect key workouts. The “eat back” trap turns a 400 kcal plan into maintenance. Judge over 14 days of weight and training quality, not one long-run screenshot.

FAQ

Should rest days be a bigger deficit?

Trim a little if truly sedentary, but keep protein steady. Huge rest-day cuts raise binge risk and can leave you flat for the next quality session.

What protein for a 70 kg runner in a cut?

About 110–155 g/day (1.6–2.2 g/kg), with carbs timed around hard runs.
