Zone 2 vs Zone 3 Running: What Changes and When to Use Each

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Zone 2 builds aerobic capacity with low recovery cost. Zone 3 is moderate “steady” effort — useful when planned, harmful when every easy run drifts there. Use each with intent, not by accident.

Ranges and Feel

  • Zone 2: ~60–70% HRR or ~65–75% max HR — conversational, smooth
  • Zone 3: ~70–80% HRR or ~75–85% max HR — “comfortably hard,” short sentences

Personalize with the Heart Rate Zones Calculator and Zone 2 Calculator. Wrong zone math → wrong decisions.

What Each Develops

Zone 2: mitochondria, capillaries, fat oxidation, durable volume. Best for base, long runs, recovery days, and high-frequency weeks.

Zone 3: aerobic strength and rhythm near half/marathon effort. Best as planned steady runs, progression finishes, or marathon-pace blocks — not as default “easy.”

When to Choose Which

  • Default Zone 2 after poor sleep, when sore, or when you already have 1–2 hard sessions that week.
  • Use Zone 3 for specific stamina: 30–45 min steady, or 2–3 controlled blocks in a long run.
  • Avoid the gray-zone trap: unplanned Zone 3 every day raises load and flattens quality sessions.

Watch Limitations

Garmin/COROS wrist HR and factory % max zones often mislabel time-in-zone on hills. Pair HR with talk test and pace from the Pace Calculator. Progress markers: faster pace at same Zone 2 HR over 4–8 weeks; less drift on long easy runs.

Same Route, Two Intensities

On a flat 50-minute loop, Zone 2 might average 5:50/km at 142 bpm with full conversation. The same loop pushed to Zone 3 at 5:25/km and 158 bpm feels productive — and costs more recovery for the next quality day. If that “medium” run happens four times a week, you no longer have easy days.

Practical Takeaway

Name the purpose before you lace up. Easy day → stay Zone 2 even if pace looks slow. Steady day → Zone 3 with a time cap. Accidental moderate is the enemy of both recovery and speed.

FAQ

Is Zone 3 wasted training?

No — overused Zone 3 is the problem. One planned steady session can bridge base and threshold work.

Should I walk to stay in Zone 2 on hills?

Yes on easy days. Walking keeps the session aerobic; grinding uphill into Zone 3 does not.
