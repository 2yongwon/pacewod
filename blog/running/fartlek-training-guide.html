Fartlek Training for Runners: Unstructured Speed That Still Builds Fitness

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Fartlek (“speed play”) mixes surges and easy running inside one continuous session. It builds fitness and race instincts without track splits — useful when you want quality without rigid intervals, or when terrain makes paced reps impractical.

Free vs Structured Fartlek

Free fartlek: accelerate to landmarks, hills, or feel — hard when you feel good, float when you don’t. Best for mental freshness and trail/road mix.

Structured fartlek: timed or distance surges with easy jog between (e.g., 8 × 1 min hard / 1 min easy). Closer to intervals, still continuous. Anchor hard segments with the Pace Calculator from recent race fitness.

Sample Sessions

  • Classic play: 40–50 min easy with 6–10 surges of 30–90 s by feel
  • Pyramid: 1-2-3-2-1 min hard, equal easy jog between
  • Landmark: hard to every other lamppost / soft trail climb for 20–25 min of play
  • Race sim: 3–4 × 3–5 min near 10K–half effort, 2 min float

Warm up 10–15 min easy; cool down the same. Cap total hard time ~10–20 minutes for most runners.

Fartlek vs Tempo vs Intervals

  • Tempo: steady threshold effort — see tempo run guide
  • Intervals: fixed reps + standing/jog recoveries — interval guide
  • Fartlek: variable speed inside continuous running; recovery is usually still moving

Use fartlek when motivation or terrain fights the track. Use tempo/intervals when you need precise stimulus and measurable progress.

Watch Reality Check

Garmin/COROS may label fartlek as “tempo” or high Training Load because average pace and HR spike. Judge success by how controlled surges felt and whether easy days stay easy afterward — not by a badge. Wrist HR lags short surges; RPE and pace lead. Cross-check zones with the HR Zones Calculator.

Practical Takeaway

Pick free play for freshness or structured timed surges when you want repeatable load. Keep surrounding days Zone 2 with the Zone 2 Calculator, and do not turn every “easy” run into accidental fartlek.

FAQ

How often should I fartlek?

Once per week as a quality day is enough for most; swap it with tempo or intervals, don’t stack all three.

Can beginners use fartlek?

Yes — keep surges short (20–40 s) and conversational between. Build from a solid easy base first.
