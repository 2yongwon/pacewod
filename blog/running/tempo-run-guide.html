Tempo Run Guide

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Tempo runs train the pace you can hold while uncomfortable but controlled — roughly lactate threshold effort. Done once a week (or as cruise intervals), they raise sustainable speed without the cost of all-out intervals.

Finding Tempo Pace

  • Often ~25–40 sec/mile slower than 5K, or near 10K–half effort depending on definition
  • Feel: “comfortably hard,” short sentences, sustainable 20–40 minutes
  • Seed from a recent race via the Pace Calculator, Pace Chart, or Race Predictor
  • HR often lands near Zone 4 / LT HR — set zones with the HR Zones Calculator

Example: 10K in 48:00 (~4:48/km) → tempo often ~4:55–5:05/km for continuous work. Adjust cooler/faster days with pace; hot days with HR/RPE.

Sample Workouts

  • Classic: 20–40 min continuous tempo after warm-up
  • Progressive: easy → moderate → last 15–20 min tempo
  • Cruise intervals: 3–5 × 8–10 min at tempo, 2 min jog
  • Tempo long run: mostly easy (easy pace), finish with 15–25 min tempo

Watch Pitfalls

Garmin/COROS may label tempo as Zone 3 or 4 depending on your basis (% max vs LTHR). Wrist HR lags on surges — pace and RPE lead; HR confirms once steady. Don’t let an “aerobic TE” badge turn a tempo into an accidental threshold blow-up.

Common Mistakes

  • Starting too fast (first 5 min should feel controlled)
  • Doing tempo on tired legs every week with no easy discipline
  • Confusing tempo with VO2 intervals — different stimulus, different recovery

Building Tempo Into the Week

Most plans need one tempo or cruise session weekly in build phases. Place it mid-week with easy running the day before and after. For half marathon prep, continuous 25–35 min tempo or 3 × 10 min cruises beat random “kinda hard” runs. Keep long runs mostly easy until late-specific blocks.

Related plans: 5K, 10K.

Practical Takeaway

One quality tempo or cruise session per week is enough for most recreational plans. Keep surrounding days easy. Build duration before chasing a faster tempo pace.

FAQ

Tempo vs threshold intervals?

Same neighborhood. Continuous tempo stresses sustained control; cruise intervals accumulate similar time with slightly less mental load.
