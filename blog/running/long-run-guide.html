The Long Run: Benefits, Pacing, and Fueling

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

The long run builds aerobic capacity, tendon durability, and race-day stamina that short workouts cannot. Treat it as a monitored session: easy effort, controlled cardiac drift, and fuel when duration crosses ~75–90 minutes.

How Long Is Long Enough?

Target time on feet, not miles:

  • 10K: 60–75 minutes
  • Half marathon: 75–105 minutes peak
  • Marathon: 90–150 minutes peak (time-based)

Add 10–15 minutes every 1–2 weeks, with a cutback every 3–4 weeks. Pair with a structured plan like the 12-week half marathon plan.

Pace Easy — Watch Cardiac Drift

Most of the run should stay conversational zone 2. On flat terrain, heart rate from minute 10 to 60 should stay within ~5% at constant pace. Example: 5:45/km for 90 minutes with HR rising from 138 to 152 bpm (~10% drift) means opening pace was too fast, heat rose, or fueling/hydration slipped. See the Zone 2 Running Guide and set caps with the Zone 2 Calculator.

Garmin and COROS show pace and HR together but do not flag drift automatically — compare 20-minute HR segments. A chest strap beats wrist optical on runs over 75 minutes. On humid days, slowing 30–45 sec/mile to hold zone 2 is correct pacing.

Easy vs Progression Long Runs

Easy: entire session zone 2 (default in base building). Progression: final 15–30 minutes at marathon or half-marathon pace after a long easy start — e.g., 50 min easy → 20 min goal half pace → 5 min cool-down.

Fueling Basics

Under 75 minutes, a solid pre-run meal is usually enough. Beyond that: 30–60g carbs per hour (gel every 30–45 min or sports drink). A 70 kg runner may burn ~700–900 kcal in 90 minutes (estimate with the Calories Calculator). Eat 40–60g carbs ~2 hours prior; take ~30g at minutes 45 and 70. After: carbs + protein within 60 minutes.

Practical Takeaway

Place the long run 24–48 hours after your last quality session, keep at least two true easy days, and stop for sharp pain, dizziness, or extreme heat. A strong long run confirms durability — not race fitness alone. Use the Race Predictor with honest recent inputs.

FAQ

Should I use pace or heart rate?

Heart rate for easy long runs; pace for progression finishes. HR accounts for heat, hills, and fatigue.

Is walking a failure?

No. Walk breaks on heat or hills often keep HR in zone 2 better than forcing run pace. Adaptation comes from time on feet at the right intensity.
