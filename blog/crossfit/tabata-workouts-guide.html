Tabata Workouts for CrossFit: 20/10 Intervals, Movement Selection, and Templates

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Classic Tabata is 20 seconds work / 10 seconds rest × 8 (4 minutes). It’s a short spike — not a full replacement for longer EMOMs or easy aerobic work. Generator: WOD Generator. Related: EMOM, running balance.

Key Points

  • Choose movements you can repeat with safe form when tired
  • Score often uses lowest-round reps — pace so round 8 doesn’t collapse
  • One Tabata finisher ≠ a full hard day; don’t stack after heavy squats + intervals
  • Running + Tabata same day: keep one easy if the other is true redline
  • 1–2 hard Tabata exposures/week is enough for most

Templates

Template 1: Classic Air Squat Tabata

  • Format20 sec max air squats / 10 sec rest × 8 rounds
  • Target14–18 reps per round; lowest round within 4 of highest
  • ScaleBox squats if depth breaks; goblet squat (light) for load progression

Template 2: Assault Bike Calorie Tabata

  • Format20 sec max cal / 10 sec rest × 8 rounds
  • Target12–16 cal per round (varies by machine and athlete)
  • ScaleRow erg or ski erg using same cal targets; recalibrate per machine

Template 3: Push and Squat Alternating Tabata

  • Rounds 1–420 sec push-ups / 10 sec rest
  • Rounds 5–820 sec air squats / 10 sec rest
  • ScaleKnee push-ups; box squats

Template 4: Double Under Tabata

  • Format20 sec max double unders / 10 sec rest × 8 rounds
  • Target30–50 reps per round for proficient athletes
  • ScaleSingle unders (count × 2 for comparison); penguin steps for beginners

Practical Takeaway

Use Tabata as a sharp 4–12 minute dose with clear scoring. Progress by raising the lowest-round count — not by adding junk volume after you’re cooked.

FAQ

Is Tabata the same as any 20/10 workout?

Popular usage says yes; classic protocol is one movement, eight rounds. Mixed circuits are fine — they’re a cousin of the study protocol.

Best movements for Tabata?

Simple, repeatable patterns: squats, calories on a bike/rower, controlled push-ups, double-unders. Avoid max-skill barbell cycling when tired.
