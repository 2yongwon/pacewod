AMRAP Workouts: Pacing, Programming, and Templates for Every Level

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

AMRAP = As Many Rounds/Reps As Possible in a fixed time. You choose breaks — pacing is the skill. Compare with EMOM; build sessions via the WOD Generator and Workout Library.

Key Points

  • Plan a sustainable round time in minute 1; negative-split the clock when possible
  • EMOM fixes work/rest; For Time races a fixed task; AMRAP maximizes work in a window
  • Cap hard AMRAPs when stacking running — running + CrossFit balance
  • Wearable calories on mixed AMRAP are noisy — treat the Calories Calculator as a planning aid
  • Retest the same piece every 4–8 weeks under identical standards

Templates

AMRAP 8 — The Foundation

  • Round8 Air Squats · 6 Push-ups (knee or box) · 10 Sit-ups

Target: 8+ rounds · 24 reps per round · scale push-ups to incline if rounds drop below 6

AMRAP 10 — Cardio Starter

  • Round10 Single Unders · 8 Walking Lunges · 6 Ring Rows or Band Pull-aparts

Target: 7–9 rounds · low impact, good for hybrid athletes on easy-run days

AMRAP 12 — The Classic Triplet

  • Round5 Pull-ups · 10 Push-ups · 15 Air Squats

Target: 8–10 rounds (240–300 reps) · benchmark against same workout monthly

AMRAP 16 — Engine + Strength

  • Round12/10 Cal Row · 8 KB Swings (24/16 kg) · 6 Box Jumps (24/20")

Target: 5–7 rounds · row pace should stay within 5% split drift across all rounds

Programming tip: place hard AMRAPs away from long runs and heavy lower-body strength when possible. If both must land near each other, scale the AMRAP’s gymnastics volume so legs and grip survive the week.

Practical Takeaway

Progress by unbroken sets and steadier round splits, not only total reps. Scale so technique survives the last two minutes. Beginner AMRAPs should prioritize unbroken basics over advanced skills under fatigue.

FAQ

How hard should minute one feel?

About 7/10 — if you’re redlining immediately, you won’t hold rounds.

Best AMRAP length for conditioning?

8–15 minutes covers most engine work. 20+ minutes is muscular-endurance pacing — program it on purpose.
