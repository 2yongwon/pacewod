CrossFit Open Strategy: Pacing Unknown Workouts and Managing Fatigue

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

The Open rewards reading a workout in five minutes, picking a break scheme that survives the middle, and still having something left. Fitness matters; strategy decides where it shows up.

Practice with the WOD Generator and Workout Library. Format depth: For Time, AMRAP, Benchmarks.

Five-Minute Read

  1. Score type (time, rounds+reps, multi-part)
  2. Limiter (grip, midline, high-failure skill)
  3. Total volume estimate
  4. Transition costs (plates, rope, equipment hops)
  5. Attempt plan (data then score, or one attempt)

Warm up the exact movements; leave 8–12 minutes calm. Second attempt only with a named fix (“5-5-5 thrusters from the start”) — not “go harder.” Prefer next-day retests for long/skill-dense pieces.

Unbroken vs Break Early

Unbroken only if the set is well inside repeatable capacity, isn’t the limiter, and breaking costs more than it saves. Break early when form decays in the first third, failure cost is high (MU, heavy cleans, HS walk), grip/breath stacks, or the format rewards splits (classic AMRAP).

Example: 15 thrusters in an AMRAP — if unbroken 15 is a fresh PR, open 8-7 or 5-5-5. Name schemes: descending 10-8-7, even 5×5 with 3–5 breaths, or disciplined singles. Time rests; “until ready” expands every round.

Score Optimization vs Survival

Scale when Rx becomes long static rests and no-reps. Optimization means standards first, rest before the limiter (not after failure), and ignoring neighbors’ break patterns. If you redline in minute four of a 20-minute AMRAP, shrink sets and rebuild a split — partial reps still count.

For Time: open ~80–85% of fantasy pace; plan the first break. AMRAP: round one slightly easy; if drift >8–10%, shrink immediately.

Between Open Weeks

48 hours after a hard attempt: sleep, protein, easy aerobic. Keep skill fresh and short; maintain strength with lower volume. Hybrid athletes: Zone 2 yes, tempo/intervals no. Rising morning RHR 5–8 bpm → cut accessory intensity. Express fitness; don’t build it with hero WODs the day after a score.

Practical Takeaway

Write the plan before the clock. Break early on the limiter, protect standards, and treat Open weeks as a peaking block — boring recovery beats “staying sharp.”

FAQ

Always two attempts?

No. Two help when attempt one revealed a fixable strategy error and you can recover. They hurt on long/skill-dense tests when the plan is just “harder.”

How to pace an unknown AMRAP?

Estimate from the hardest movement, open 5–10% slower than fantasy, reassess at one-third and two-thirds, shrink sets if splits drift ~8–10%+.
