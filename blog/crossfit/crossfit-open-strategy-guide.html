CrossFit Open Strategy: Pacing Unknown Workouts and Managing Fatigue

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Wanting unbroken Rx sets and needing a score that survives the middle often conflict. The Open rewards a five-minute read and a break scheme you can hold. Practice: WOD Generator, Workout Library; formats: For Time, AMRAP, Benchmarks.

Five-Minute Read

  1. Score type (time, rounds+reps, multi-part)
  2. Limiter (grip, midline, high-failure skill)
  3. Total volume estimate + transition costs
  4. Attempt plan (data then score, or one attempt)

Numeric scenario: AMRAP with 15 thrusters/round. If unbroken 15 is a fresh PR, open 8-7 or 5-5-5. Round-1 pace ~5–10% slower than fantasy; if split drift >8–10% by one-third, shrink sets immediately. For Time: open ~80–85% of fantasy pace and plan the first break.

Expert Note

Second attempts help only with a named fix (“5-5-5 from the start”) — not “go harder.” Prefer next-day retests for long/skill-dense pieces.

Decision rule: if form decays in the first third or failure cost is high (MU, heavy cleans), break early by plan — don’t wait for the first no-rep cascade.

Between Open Weeks

48 hours after a hard attempt: sleep, protein, easy aerobic. Hybrid athletes: Zone 2 yes, tempo/intervals no. Rising morning RHR 5–8 bpm → cut accessory intensity. Related: recovery between workouts.

Heat Strategy and Standards

Warm up the exact movements; leave 8–12 minutes calm so the first minute isn’t a surprise. Film or use a judge mindset: range of motion failures don’t count, and “extra” messy reps aren’t fitness. Ignore neighboring athletes’ break schemes unless they match your limiter. Between weeks, keep skill fresh and short; maintain strength with lower volume. Hybrid runners: easy aerobic yes, interval track sessions no. Express fitness on the test; don’t try to build it with random heroes the next morning.

Write break schemes on paper or tape. In the moment, adrenaline erases vague plans. If you redline in minute four of a long AMRAP, shrink sets and rebuild a split — partial rounds still count. Standards first, neighbors never.

Practical Takeaway

Write the plan before the clock. Break early on the limiter, protect standards, and treat Open weeks as peaking — not hero-WOD season.

FAQ

Always two attempts?

No. Two help when attempt one revealed a fixable strategy error and you can recover. They hurt when the plan is just “harder.”
