Gymnastics Skills for CrossFit: Pull-Ups, HSPU, Muscle-Ups, and Progressions

Updated August 2026 · 12 min read

CrossFit gymnastics fails more athletes on volume management than on talent. The kip that looks efficient in a set of 5 becomes a shoulder shredder across 100 reps of Murph when strict strength and scapular control were never built. Treat skill acquisition like an engineering problem: prerequisites, progression gates, volume caps, and deliberate practice under low fatigue — then expose the skill to metcon stress.

This guide covers the strict → kipping → butterfly pull-up path, handstand push-up progressions from wall walks to kipping HSPU, muscle-up prerequisites, and weekly volume limits that keep elbows and shoulders online. Scale intelligently with How to Scale CrossFit Workouts. Practice density with EMOM templates. Generate skill-compatible sessions via the WOD Generator or browse the Workout Library.

Principles Before Progressions

  • Strict before dynamic. Kipping multiplies force through positions you already own. It does not replace strength.
  • Positions before volume. Ten perfect reps beat fifty ugly ones for skill acquisition — and for tendon health.
  • Fresh practice, fatigued application. Learn at the start of sessions. Test under fatigue only after the pattern is stable when fresh.
  • Volume has a budget. Elbows and shoulders adapt slower than your ego. Cap hard hanging and pressing volume, especially when adding a new skill.
Expert Note

If you cannot hold a dead hang for 30–40 seconds with active shoulders (depression and slight retraction, not passive shrugging into the ears), you are not ready for high-volume kipping. Spend 3–4 weeks on hang time, scap pull-ups, and strict pulling before chasing butterfly cadence. Metcons will still get done — use ring rows and banded options from the scaling guide while the base builds.

Pull-Ups: Strict → Kipping → Butterfly

Stage 1 — Strict Pull-Up Strength

Gate to advance: 3–5 unbroken strict pull-ups from a dead hang with chin clearly over the bar, controlled lower (≤3 sec eccentric). If you are below this gate, prioritize:

  • Scapular pull-ups: 3 × 6–10
  • Banded strict pull-ups or foot-assisted negatives: 4 × 3–5
  • Ring rows at challenging angle: 3 × 8–12
  • Dead hangs: 3 × 20–40 sec

Volume note: 40–60 quality strict pulling reps per session, 2–3× weekly, is enough for most developing athletes. Adding Cindy Rx on top of that same day is how elbows get angry.

Stage 2 — Kipping Pull-Ups

Learn the hollow → arch swing on the bar without pulling. Two to three sessions of swing-only drills beat jumping into full kips immediately. Then add the pull at the back of the swing so the hips help the chin over the bar.

Gate to advance: 8–12 unbroken kipping pull-ups with consistent rhythm and no excessive elbow flare or incomplete chin height. Cadence target for workout use: roughly 1.0–1.3 seconds per rep once efficient. Faster is not always better if positions degrade.

EMOM skill builder: EMOM 8–10: 3–5 kipping pull-ups + 20 sec hollow hold. Must keep ≥25 seconds rest. If rest disappears, cut reps — see EMOM guidance.

Stage 3 — Butterfly Pull-Ups

Butterfly is a continuous circular cadence — efficient for high-volume workouts when timing is locked, destructive when it is not. Prerequisites: comfortable kipping sets of 15+, solid grip, and no sharp elbow or shoulder pain historically with kipping volume.

Train butterfly in small sets (3–5 reps) for several weeks before using it in Fran or Cindy. Gate for metcon use: 15+ unbroken butterfly when fresh with chin height meeting standard every rep. If reps shorten or timing desyncs by rep 8, stay with regular kip for workouts and keep butterfly in skill blocks only.

Chest-to-Bar

C2B demands more pull height — typically more strict strength and a slightly different kip timing. Do not chase C2B volume until chin-over-bar kipping sets of 15–20 are easy. Scale C2B to regular pull-ups or jumping C2B with controlled eccentrics when standards matter less than stimulus.

Handstand Push-Ups: Wall Walk to Kip

Progression Ladder

  1. Pike push-ups (floor): build pressing strength. Target 3 × 8–12.
  2. Elevated-pike push-ups (feet on box): increase inversion. Target 3 × 6–10.
  3. Wall walks: 3 × 2–4 walks to a consistent height; nose-to-wall holds 3 × 20–40 sec for midline.
  4. Box HSPU (hands on floor, feet on box): closest strength pattern to HSPU without full wall freestanding demand.
  5. Strict HSPU (kipping not yet): abmat as needed; build to 3 × 3–5 strict.
  6. Kipping HSPU: only after strict singles are reliable and shoulder warm-ups are non-negotiable.

Volume and Risk Management

HSPU volume spikes are a top driver of shoulder irritation in CrossFit. Cap new HSPU trainees at ~20–30 quality reps per session for the first month of wall work, not counting warm-up pike push-ups. Advanced athletes handling Diane-level volume should still avoid max-effort HSPU practice the day before heavy overhead strength.

Pacing example — Diane HSPU: If strict capacity is 5 unbroken fresh, do not open Diane with unbroken 21s. Plan 5-5-5-3-3 or tripoding early. Time math: 45 HSPU at 3 sec/rep + breaks ≈ 135 sec bar/wall time minimum; with realistic breaks, budget 4–6 minutes of the workout for HSPU alone. If that blows the time domain, scale to pike or box HSPU per the scaling guide.

Expert Note

Wrist and shoulder prep is training, not optional fluff. Two to three minutes of wrist rocks, scapular CARs, and light dumbbell pressing before HSPU practice reduces the "surprise" pain that appears at week three of a gymnastics block. Skip prep when short on time and you are choosing which debt to pay later — usually with time off.

Muscle-Ups: Prerequisites Before Ambition

Muscle-ups fail when athletes skip prerequisites. The movement is a pull to a transition to a dip — each piece must exist independently.

Bar Muscle-Up Prerequisites

  • 5–8 strict pull-ups OR 10–15 solid kipping/C2B pull-ups
  • 8–10 parallel-bar or ring dips (support strength)
  • Competent hip pop / kip that elevates the chest toward the bar
  • No sharp anterior shoulder pain with pulling

Ring Muscle-Up Prerequisites

  • All bar MU prerequisites, plus
  • False grip hangs and pull-ups (rings): 3 × 5–8 false-grip ring rows progressing to pulls
  • Stable support hold on rings: 3 × 15–20 sec
  • Ring dips: 5+ unbroken

Progression Drills

  • Low-ring muscle-up transitions (feet assisted)
  • Jumping bar muscle-ups with slow negatives
  • Pull to belly / pull to transition height on bar without committing to the sit-through until consistent
  • Dip lockouts from the bottom with assistance as needed

Gate for workout inclusion: 1–3 unbroken muscle-ups when fresh with confident transition. Until then, substitute in metcons: pull-up + dip supersets matching intended MU count, or jumping MUs with controlled eccentrics. Forcing 12 muscle-ups into a workout when you own one messy rep teaches panic transitions, not skill.

Muscle-Up Volume Limits

Skill days: 10–20 quality attempts max (including drills), not 50 failed kips. Workout days: only prescribe volumes you can complete in sets of 1–3 without form collapse. Elbow tendons often complain 24–72 hours later — if next-morning elbow pain appears, cut MU volume 50% for 1–2 weeks and emphasize eccentrics and blood-flow work.

Toes-to-Bar and Core-to-Bar Capacity

T2B are a gymnastics-core hybrid. Progression: hanging knee raises → knees-to-elbow → toes-to-bar with controlled rhythm. Gate for large workout volumes (e.g., 75–100 T2B in a chipper): 10–15 unbroken when fresh. Below that, break into sets of 3–5 from the start or scale to knees-to-chest.

Grip again limits T2B late in chippers. Open hands on planned breaks; do not chalk every three reps. Pair T2B skill EMOMs with non-grip minutes (e.g., Min 1: 4–6 T2B, Min 2: 8 goblet squats) to avoid forearm blow-up mid-practice.

Weekly Volume Budgets

Approximate caps for athletes adding skill volume on top of normal CrossFit classes. Reduce if pain, poor sleep, or concurrent heavy Olympic lifting.

  • Pulling (all hang variations combined): 150–250 quality reps/week for intermediates; beginners 80–150. Murph week is an outlier — cut other pulling that week by 50%+.
  • HSPU / vertical press gymnastics: 40–80 quality reps/week while learning; 80–120 when established. Exclude easy pike warm-ups from the "quality" count only if they stay light.
  • Muscle-up attempts: 30–50/week including drills for developing athletes; advanced athletes still rarely need 100+ outside specific competition prep.
  • Hard hanging days: Cap at 3× weekly. Separate by at least 48 hours when possible if elbows are sensitive.

Heart Rate Context During Skill Work

Skill practice should mostly sit in zone 1–2. If your chest strap shows zone 4 during "technique" butterfly work, you are conditioning, not learning — cut density, add rest between sets to 60–90 seconds, and save the redline for metcons. Use the HR Zone Calculator to set anchors. Wearables that flag high Training Load after a skill day usually mean you turned practice into a covert workout.

Sample Skill Blocks

Pull-Up Focus — 12 Minutes

  • Min 0–4Scap pulls + 2 × max dead hang (cap 40 sec)
  • Min 4–9EMOM 5: 3–5 strict or kipping pull-ups (stage-appropriate)
  • Min 9–12Ring rows 2 × 10 or light band face pulls

Then run the class metcon scaled to protect elbows if volume was high

HSPU Focus — 12 Minutes

  • PrepWrist + scap + 2 wall walks
  • Main5 × 3 pike or strict HSPU @ RPE 7 · 60–90 sec rest
  • AccessoryDB seated press 2 × 8–10 light

Muscle-Up Focus — 15 Minutes

  • DrillsFalse grip / low ring transitions 8–10 min
  • AttemptsMax 8–12 quality MU attempts or jumping MUs

Stop early if transition quality drops — failed volume is not productive volume

Integrating Skills With Strength and Metcons

Order of operations for a typical day: warm-up → skill (fresh) → strength if programmed → metcon with appropriate scaling. Do not place max muscle-up attempts after a grip-intensive chipper. For weekly structure — how many hard gymnastics days, how to space them from heavy pressing — use the Weekly Programming Guide.

Hybrid runners should be especially conservative with high-volume kipping in the 48 hours before long runs if shoulders already carry fatigue from swimming or other sports — systemic recovery is shared. FAQ

How long does it take to get a first pull-up or muscle-up?

Highly individual. Many athletes move from zero strict pull-ups to 3–5 with 6–12 weeks of consistent 2–3× weekly pulling practice. First muscle-ups often take additional months after pull-up and dip standards are met. Athletes who skip strict work and only kip sometimes "get" a muscle-up sooner and stall harder — prerequisites still matter for sustainable volume.

Should I do butterfly pull-ups in every workout once I learn them?

No. Butterfly is a tool for high-volume, unbroken sets when timing is reliable. Use regular kipping when fatigued, when volume is moderate, or when butterfly timing desyncs. Many advanced athletes still choose chin-over-bar kipping in mixed-modal pieces to save shoulders and preserve accuracy.

Why do my elbows hurt after toes-to-bar and pull-ups?

Usually cumulative tendon load from hanging volume, aggressive kipping, and insufficient recovery between hanging days. Cut hanging volume 40–50% for 1–2 weeks, keep pain-free strict work, add eccentric-focused rows, and space hanging sessions 48 hours apart. Sharp pain warrants professional assessment — do not "train through" joint-line sharp pain.

Can I learn HSPU and muscle-ups in the same block?

Possible but suboptimal for most intermediates. Both stress the shoulders heavily. Prioritize one primary skill for 4–6 weeks while maintaining the other at low volume (e.g., muscle-up focus with 2 × 5 pike push-ups for maintenance). Dual-maxing skill volume is a common overreach pattern — see overtraining signals in Overtraining Signs for Runners which translate well to CrossFit recovery debt.
