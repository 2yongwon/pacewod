CrossFit Benchmark WODs: Fran, Cindy, Murph, and How to Pace Them

Updated August 2026 · 12 min read

Benchmark WODs are the standardized tests of functional fitness — same reps, same movements, same name, retested months apart to measure progress. Fran destroys lungs in under five minutes. Cindy grinds for twenty. Murph breaks egos on Memorial Day and every other day someone decides to "just try it."

Each benchmark has a personality: sprint, grind, or hero. Pacing strategy that works on Fran will bury you on Murph. Retesting without a plan produces one number and no insight — retesting with rep targets and split awareness shows whether engine, strength, or transition efficiency improved.

This guide breaks down Fran, Cindy, Murph, and pacing principles that apply across the benchmark library — plus target times, scaling, and common mistakes.

Why Benchmarks Matter

Random daily WODs build general fitness. Benchmarks measure it. When you repeat Fran three months apart with identical scaling, a 45-second improvement means something — your thruster capacity, pull-up endurance, or mental pacing got better. Without fixed tests, progress hides inside varied workouts.

Benchmarks also calibrate expectations. A beginner who finishes Murph in 55 minutes with a weight vest did not "fail" because an elite athlete posted 40 — they established a baseline to beat. Log the score, the scaling, and how you felt. Retest on the same version.

Need a different workout today? Use the WOD Generator — benchmarks are for tracking, not daily training variety.

Expert Note

Never retest a benchmark cold. One practice attempt at 80–90% effort two weeks before a retest reveals pacing mistakes without full recovery cost. First-time benchmarks are for learning splits; second attempts are for scores.

Fran — The Sprint Benchmark

Rx: 21-15-9 reps of thrusters (95/65 lb) and pull-ups, for time.

Fran is a two-movement sprint lasting 3–10 minutes for most athletes. Thrusters combine a front squat and push press — legs and shoulders under metabolic stress. Pull-ups add grip and lat fatigue exactly when breathing is hardest.

Target Times by Level

  • Beginner (scaled): 8–12 min — PVC or 45/35 lb thrusters, jumping pull-ups or ring rows
  • Intermediate: 5–8 min — full pull-ups, 75/55 lb or Rx weight with broken sets
  • Advanced: 3–5 min — unbroken or near-unbroken sets, aggressive transitions
  • Elite: sub-2:30 — Games-level engine and barbell cycling

Pacing Fran

The classic mistake: sprint the 21 thrusters, redline before pull-ups start, then grind broken sets for ten minutes. Better approach:

  1. 21 thrusters: Break 11-10 or 7-7-7 from the start — never unbroken unless your max unbroken at Rx is 30+ with fresh legs. First set should feel controlled, not heroic.
  2. 21 pull-ups: Sets of 7-7-7 or 6-5-4-3-3. Rest 3–5 seconds between sets, not 30. Long rests on pull-ups kill Fran time.
  3. 15 thrusters: 8-7 or 5-5-5. Barbell should not touch the ground between sets — hand-to-hand on the bar saves 15+ seconds total.
  4. 15 pull-ups: 5-5-5 or 4-4-4-3. Grip management starts here — shake out briefly, not fully.
  5. 9 thrusters: Single set if possible; if not, 5-4 without dropping from the rack position.
  6. 9 pull-ups: Whatever remains — often 5-4 or 3-3-3.

Transition tax: Walk between bar and pull-up bar, not sprint. Ten seconds saved on thrusters lost to a 5-second transition gap is net zero. Setup both stations before starting the clock.

Fran Scaling

  • Reduce weight before reducing reps — 21-15-9 at 65/45 lb preserves stimulus better than 15-12-9 at Rx.
  • Jumping pull-ups or banded pull-ups maintain rep scheme integrity for retesting.
  • Thruster weight should allow sets of 7+ when fresh — if 5 reps is max, weight is too heavy for benchmark pacing.

Cindy — The Grind Benchmark

Rx: AMRAP 20 minutes of 5 pull-ups, 10 push-ups, 15 air squats.

Cindy tests muscular endurance and transition speed over twenty minutes — the opposite pacing problem from Fran. Start too fast and round count collapses in the final five minutes; start too slow and rounds are left on the table.

Target Rounds by Level

  • Beginner (scaled): 8–12 rounds — ring rows, knee push-ups, air squats
  • Intermediate: 14–18 rounds — full push-ups, pull-ups in sets of 3–5
  • Advanced: 19–24 rounds — mostly unbroken pull-ups early, steady push-up sets
  • Elite: 25+ rounds — sub-45 second rounds sustained

Pacing Cindy

Think in rounds per minute, not total rounds. Target round time:

  • Beginner: 90–120 sec/round → 10–13 rounds
  • Intermediate: 65–85 sec/round → 14–18 rounds
  • Advanced: 50–60 sec/round → 20–24 rounds

Minute 1–5: Should feel almost easy. Pull-ups in 2 sets max, push-ups unbroken, squats unbroken. If minute 1 requires a gasping break on push-ups, you will fade by minute 12.

Minute 6–15: Steady state. Pull-ups become 3-2 or 2-2-1. Push-ups 5-5 or 6-4. Squats stay unbroken — they are recovery for upper body, not a bottleneck.

Minute 16–20: Hold round structure. Do not sprint the final round unless you have 2+ rounds in reserve — athletes who empty the tank at 17:00 often fail to complete another full round.

Expert Note

Cindy's push-ups are the hidden limiter. Athletes who can do 30 unbroken push-ups fresh often hit 5-5-3-2 by minute 10 in Cindy. Practice 10-rep sets on the minute for push-up endurance two weeks before a retest — more valuable than extra pull-up volume for this benchmark.

Cindy Scaling

  • Ring rows at a angle that allows 5 reps in one set when fresh.
  • Hand-release push-ups or hands-elevated push-ups preserve ROM without knee scaling if shoulder allows.
  • Log scaled version consistently — "Cindy (ring rows)" is a different test than Rx Cindy.

Murph — The Hero Benchmark

Rx: For time — 1 mile run, 100 pull-ups, 200 push-ups, 300 air squats, 1 mile run. Optional 20/14 lb weight vest.

Murph is 45–75+ minutes of work for most athletes. It combines running, high-rep gymnastics, and mental monotony. Memorial Day crowds make it social; solo Murph requires even stricter pacing discipline.

Target Times by Level

  • Beginner (no vest, partitioned): 50–70 min — run/walk, ring rows, knee push-ups
  • Intermediate (no vest): 40–55 min — partitioned 20 rounds of Cindy structure
  • Advanced (vest or no vest): 35–45 min with vest; 30–38 min without
  • Elite: sub-40 min with vest — top Games athletes sub-35

Pacing Murph: The Partition Strategy

Rx unpartitioned Murph (all pull-ups, then all push-ups, then all squats) is a grip and pec death trap for anyone without Games-level capacity. Standard partition:

20 rounds of: 5 pull-ups, 10 push-ups, 15 air squats — then run bookends.

  • Start1 mile run — controlled, not sprint (7:00–9:00/mi pace for most)
  • Round 1–5Steady — pull-ups unbroken, push-ups 10, squats unbroken
  • Round 6–10Pull-ups 3-2, push-ups 5-5, squats unbroken
  • Round 11–15Same structure — do not accelerate yet
  • Round 16–20Hold splits; optional micro-push on final 2 rounds
  • Finish1 mile run — expect legs of concrete; shuffle if needed

First mile should feel conservative. A 6:30 opening mile on a 45-minute Murph athlete adds 90 seconds of recovery debt before 100 pull-ups begin.

Weight Vest Considerations

A 20 lb vest adds roughly 3–8 minutes for intermediate athletes — more on the runs, less on squats. Vest Murph is a different benchmark than no-vest; never compare scores directly. Running with vest requires prior exposure — first vest Murph should use 14 lb or no vest to learn partition pacing.

Hybrid runners doing Murph: treat the runs as easy aerobic effort, not race pace. Murph the day before a long run destroys both sessions. Schedule 48+ hours from quality running, as covered in our running and CrossFit balance guide.

Murph Scaling

  • Half Murph: 800m run, 50-100-150, 800m run — valid entry point.
  • Ring rows and incline push-ups with full rep counts preserve the test structure.
  • Partition into 10 rounds of 10-20-30 if 20 rounds feels administratively heavy — same total reps.

Other Key Benchmarks — Quick Pacing Notes

Grace (30 clean and jerks for time, 135/95 lb)

Sprint benchmark like Fran. Singles or quick doubles from the start — touch-and-go sets of 5 fall apart after rep 15. Target: 3–6 min intermediate. Barbell cycling efficiency dominates.

Helen (3 rounds: 400m run, 21 KB swings, 12 pull-ups)

Hybrid sprint — ~8–12 min intermediate. Run hard but not all-out; pull-ups break by round 2. Consistent round splits beat a fast round 1.

Isabel (30 snatches for time, 135/95 lb)

Power endurance test. Singles or doubles only for most Rx athletes. Sub-3 min requires proficient touch-and-go — do not attempt TnG without practice at fatigue.

DT (5 rounds: 12 deadlifts, 9 hang power cleans, 6 push jerks, 155/105 lb)

Barbell complex benchmark — 10–15 min intermediate. Unbroken complex ideal but unrealistic for most; plan 6-6 / 5-4 / 3-3 per movement per round from the start.

Universal Benchmark Pacing Principles

  1. Classify the WOD: Under 5 min = sprint (Fran, Grace). 5–15 min = threshold (Helen). 15+ min = grind (Cindy, Murph). Pacing strategy follows duration, not movement count alone.
  2. Pre-planned breaks beat reactive breaks: Decide rep splits before the clock starts. Reactive breaking defaults to too-long rests.
  3. Transitions are free time: Setup equipment, chalk, and station proximity before 3-2-1-GO. Murph and Fran both punish lazy transitions.
  4. First round is a probe, not a race: Round 1 at 95% effort reveals true sustainable pace. Round 1 at 110% reveals your bonk point.
  5. Log everything: Time, scaling, rep splits per round, RPE. Benchmarks without notes are single data points, not training tools.

Benchmark Retest Schedule

Retest the same benchmark every 8–12 weeks — not weekly. Too frequent retesting becomes the training program, not a measure of it. Align retests with deload weeks when fresh:

  • Fran / Grace / Isabel: After 1–2 easy days, not after heavy squat week.
  • Cindy: Any day with fresh push-ups and pull-ups — avoid day after bench or high-volume pressing.
  • Murph: Standalone session, no other training that day. May need 3–5 days reduced volume afterward.

Common Benchmark Mistakes

  • Rx ego: Rx Fran in 12 minutes teaches bad pacing. Scaled fast beats Rx slow for learning.
  • No partition plan on Murph: 30-60-90 unpartitioned is a different workout than 20× Cindy rounds — and usually slower for non-elites.
  • Ignoring grip on pull-up benchmarks: Fresh grip tests ≠ fatigued grip tests. Train pull-ups after rowing or deadlifts before retesting Fran.
  • Comparing across gyms: Run distance, pull-up standards, and vest weight vary. Compare your own retests only.
  • Benchmark as daily WOD: Murph every month does not build Murph fitness — structured training between retests does.

Training Between Retests

Improve Fran by improving thruster efficiency and pull-up density — EMOM thrusters and EMOM pull-up work build both. Improve Cindy with push-up volume on the minute and AMRAP practice at 80% effort. Improve Murph with long Zone 2 runs plus partitioned gymnastics volume — not by running Murph weekly.

Estimate Murph run pacing with our Pace Calculator — if your easy mile pace is 9:00, opening Murph at 8:00 guarantees a survival shuffle on the closing mile.

Final Takeaway

Fran rewards controlled aggression and pre-planned rep splits. Cindy rewards patience in the first five minutes and push-up endurance. Murph rewards partition discipline and conservative opening miles. Classify each benchmark by duration, plan breaks before you start, and retest on the same scaling every 8–12 weeks.

Benchmarks are mirrors — run them with a strategy, log the splits, and let the numbers tell you what to train next.

FAQ

How often should I retest benchmark WODs?

Every 8–12 weeks for the same benchmark — enough time for training adaptations to show. Sprint benchmarks (Fran, Grace) can retest on fresh days with minimal recovery cost. Hero WODs like Murph are quarterly events, not monthly, because recovery demand is high. Log scaling identically each time or the comparison is meaningless.

Should I partition Murph or do it straight through?

Partition (20 rounds of 5-10-15) is faster and more sustainable for nearly all athletes below elite level. Straight-through 100 pull-ups first destroys grip before push-ups begin. Unpartitioned Murph is a valid Rx option for athletes who can complete 30+ unbroken pull-ups when fresh and 50+ push-ups in sets of 15 — otherwise partition is smarter pacing, not scaling.

What is a good Fran time for an intermediate athlete?

5–8 minutes at Rx weight with full pull-ups defines solid intermediate territory. Sub-5 enters advanced range; 8–12 minutes suggests either scaling is appropriate or pull-up/thruster density needs work before pushing Rx weight. Compare against your own retest trend, not leaderboard posts — regional variance in pull-up and thruster standards affects times.

Can I do benchmark WODs if I also train for running races?

Yes, with spacing. Sprint benchmarks (Fran, Helen) fit 48 hours before a quality run if legs are recovered. Murph and other 45+ minute hero WODs need 3–5 days before a long run or hard interval session. During race-specific blocks, maintain fitness with scaled or shortened benchmarks rather than all-out retests — a 75% Murph practice preserves feel without the recovery tax of a PR attempt.
