CrossFit Weekly Programming: How to Structure Strength, Metcons, and Rest

Updated August 2026 · 12 min read

Random daily WODs can make you fitter for a while. They also produce the classic pattern: three hard days in a row, a surprise hero workout on day four, then a week of flat performance you blame on "aging" or "diet." Programming is just fatigue accounting. Strength, metcons, skills, and rest occupy the same recovery budget — spend it deliberately.

This guide gives sample week templates for 3-, 4-, 5-, and 6-day schedules, intensity distribution targets, hard-day spacing rules, and hybrid modifications for runners. Pair with Running and CrossFit Training Balance. Watch for accumulated fatigue using patterns described in Overtraining Signs for Runners. Build sessions with the WOD Generator and the Workout Library.

The Weekly Budget: What You Are Actually Allocating

Think in three currencies: neural/strength stress, metabolic/conditioning stress, and local tissue stress (grip, shoulders, Achilles, lumbar). A heavy squat day spends neural and lumbar currency. A 20-minute barbell AMRAP spends metabolic plus grip. A long run spends metabolic plus lower-limb tissue. Two "different" hard days that spend the same currency still stack.

Intensity Distribution Target

For most non-competitive CrossFit athletes training 4–5 days weekly:

  • ~70–80% easy/moderate sessions: technique, aerobic metcons at sustainable pace, accessory strength, Zone 2 cardio
  • ~15–25% hard sessions: heavy strength (≥80% 1RM), race-pace benchmarks, high-power For Time pieces
  • ~5%+ very hard / test days: max effort benchmarks, competitions — infrequent

If every class feels like RPE 9, your distribution is inverted. You are collecting fatigue, not adaptations. Use chest-strap average HR and session RPE together: a "hard" metcon should push average HR into zone 4–5 for short pieces or high zone 3–4 for 15–20 minutes. Easy days should mostly stay zone 2 — set zones with the HR Zone Calculator and Zone 2 Calculator.

Expert Note

Wearable "recovery" scores are useful trend tools, not veto buttons for every session. If HRV trends down for 5–7 days, resting HR is elevated 5+ bpm, and workouts feel flat, cut intensity for 3–5 days — keep easy movement. One low score after poor sleep is noise. Chronic low scores plus declining Fran times and hang capacity are signal. See overtraining signs for the broader checklist.

Hard-Day Spacing Rules

  1. Separate max lower-body strength and high-volume squatting/lunging metcons by 48+ hours when possible.
  2. Separate heavy Olympic lifting and high-cycle barbell metcons (Fran/Isabel style) by at least one day — see Barbell Cycling Guide.
  3. Cap hard gymnastics volume days (muscle-ups, HSPU dense work) at 2–3× weekly with 48 hours between when elbows/shoulders are sensitive — Gymnastics Skills Guide.
  4. Do not stack a long run the same day as a lower-body biased hero WOD unless you are specifically peaking for a hybrid event and accepting the cost.
  5. After a true test day (benchmark PR attempt), schedule 24–48 hours of easy work — technique, Zone 2, mobility — not another chipper.

Sample Week: 3 Training Days

Best for beginners, busy professionals, or athletes prioritizing another sport. Full rest or easy Zone 2 walks on off days.

  • MonStrength lower (squat focus 5×3–5) + short metcon 8–12 min (AMRAP or EMOM)
  • WedStrength upper / weightlifting + gymnastics skill 10–12 min + moderate metcon 12–15 min
  • FriConditional piece For Time or longer AMRAP 16–20 min + light accessory

Optional Sat: 30–45 min Zone 2 easy run or row · Sun rest

Intensity math: One hard day (Fri race-pace), one strength-priority day (Mon), one mixed moderate day (Wed). Avoid turning all three into hero WODs. Scale metcons so finish times hit intended domains — scaling framework.

Sample Week: 4 Training Days

The sweet spot for most recreational CrossFit athletes who also want a life.

  • MonHeavy squat or deadlift · short metcon ≤10 min · low eccentric legs if running Tue
  • TueRest or Zone 2 30–40 min · optional easy gymnastics skill
  • WedOlympic lifting technique + moderate loads · pull-up/HSPU skill · metcon 12–15 min
  • ThuRest or mobility / easy aerobic
  • FriStrength upper or light lower · harder For Time / benchmark-style 8–15 min
  • SatLonger engine day: chipper, 20-min AMRAP, or aerobic intervals · keep loads moderate
  • SunFull rest

Hard days: Mon (strength), Fri (metcon), Sat (engine) — spaced with recovery buffers

Format mix across the week: one AMRAP, one For Time, one EMOM or skill EMOM, optional chipper on Saturday. Variety prevents local overuse better than repeating Fran variants four times.

Sample Week: 5 Training Days

Requires honest easy days inside the gym — not five redline classes.

  • MonLower strength heavy · metcon short and sharp (≤8 min)
  • TueGymnastics skill focus · aerobic metcon Zone 3 (row/bike/ski biased) 16–20 min
  • WedOlympic lifting · light accessory · optional very short partner or skill piece
  • ThuRest or Zone 2 only
  • FriUpper strength / push-pull · classic couplet For Time
  • SatMixed-modal engine or benchmark retest · then deload next week if tested hard
  • SunRest or easy walk

Tue and Wed must stay truly moderate — if Wed becomes a hidden hero WOD, drop to 4 days

Pacing example for Tue aerobic metcon: AMRAP 18 of 12/10 cal row + 10 wall balls + 8 hang power cleans at ≤40% clean 1RM. Target round splits within 5–8% drift; average HR zone 3. If cleans force singles and HR hits zone 5 by minute 6, the load is wrong for an "aerobic" day — scale immediately.

Sample Week: 6 Training Days

Only for athletes with strong recovery habits, low outside stress, and the discipline to keep 2–3 sessions easy. This is not six Fran attempts.

  • MonHeavy lower · short metcon
  • TueSkill + Zone 2/3 monostructural 30–40 min (no barbell)
  • WedOlympic lifting + moderate metcon
  • ThuActive recovery: mobility, light gymnastics drills, easy bike 20–30 min
  • FriUpper strength · race-pace metcon
  • SatLonger conditioning or hybrid brick (see below)
  • SunFull rest — non-negotiable

If Sun rest gets skipped twice in a month, you do not have a 6-day program — you have a recovery problem

Strength + Metcon Same Day: Order and Dosing

Default order: skill (if any) → strength → metcon. Strength quality drops if you redline first; skill quality drops if you grip-out first. Exceptions: occasional fasted-engine days or competition simulations that intentionally reverse order.

  • Heavy strength (≤6 reps, ≥80%): Metcon after should be ≤10–12 minutes, limited eccentric leg damage, loads ≤45% for barbell cycling
  • Moderate strength (6–10 reps): Metcon 12–16 minutes acceptable
  • Light technique strength: Metcon can be the priority piece (16–20 min)

Total session time target: 60–75 minutes including warm-up for most athletes. Two-hour "strength then Murph" days are special events, not weekly defaults.

Hybrid: Adding Running to the Week

Full decision trees live in Running and CrossFit Training Balance. Core rules for the templates above:

  • Long run: Place on the least leg-damaging day — typically after a rest day or after upper-biased CrossFit. Avoid the morning after heavy squats + box jump metcons.
  • Quality run (intervals/tempo): Separate from heavy lower and high-volume lunging by 48 hours when possible.
  • Easy Zone 2 runs: Can replace Tue/Thu aerobic CrossFit slots — do not stack easy run + hard metcon "because the run was easy."
  • Brick example (advanced): Sat: 20-min moderate AMRAP (light loads) + 30–40 min easy run. Keep AMRAP RPE ≤7.

4-day CrossFit + 3-run hybrid sketch:

  • MonCF strength + short metcon
  • TueEasy Zone 2 run 40–50 min
  • WedCF weightlifting + skill + moderate metcon
  • ThuRun quality OR rest (not both quality run and hard CF)
  • FriCF upper + For Time
  • SatLong run
  • SunRest or optional CF engine with step-ups not box jumps
Expert Note

Hybrid athletes fail by counting days instead of currencies. "I only did CrossFit four times and ran three times" can still mean five high-intensity sessions if every CF day was RPE 9 and Tuesday's run was tempo. Label each day Easy / Moderate / Hard before the week starts. Cap Hard at 2–3 total across both sports. If you need a fourth Hard, delete one — do not squeeze it in.

Deload and Retest Rhythm

Every 4–6 weeks, insert a deload: cut metcon intensity and strength volume ~40–50% for 5–7 days while keeping frequency or slightly reducing it. Retest a benchmark (Benchmark WODs Guide) at the end of a fresh week, not at the end of a hero-WOD binge.

Track three weekly KPIs: (1) number of Hard days completed, (2) average sleep hours, (3) one repeatable output metric (e.g., 2K row split or Fran scaled time). If output falls while Hard days stay high, the program is overreaching — cut intensity before adding supplements and random recovery gadgets.

Choosing Formats Across the Week

  • Monday short piece: EMOM or short For Time — controlled density after weekend
  • Midweek: Skill EMOM + AMRAP for pacing practice
  • Friday: For Time couplet — race simulation when relatively fresh
  • Saturday: Chipper or long AMRAP — engine

Tabata and high-density intervals belong sparingly — they are hard currency. See Tabata Workouts Guide and Hero WODs Guide for when long suffering pieces are appropriate (rarely weekly).

Putting It Together

Pick the lowest frequency you can stay consistent with for 8–12 weeks. A completed 3-day template beats a theoretical 6-day template with missed sessions and junk intensity. Use the WOD Generator to fill slots with the right format, browse the Workout Library for proven pieces, and scale so each day spends the currency you budgeted — not the currency your ego prefers.

FAQ

Is 5–6 days of CrossFit too much?

Not inherently — too much intensity is the problem. Five days with two hard sessions and three easy/moderate sessions can be sustainable. Five days of RPE 8–9 metcons is not. If performance, sleep, or mood declines across 2+ weeks, reduce hard days first, then frequency. Cross-check symptoms with the overtraining guide.

Should strength and metcon be on separate days?

Ideal for pure strength goals: yes. For general CrossFit fitness: same-day strength then metcon works well if the metcon is dosed correctly (shorter and lighter after heavy lower). Separating them onto different days is better when both sessions need to be hard — otherwise one will steal recovery from the other.

How do I add running without losing CrossFit progress?

Protect one quality CrossFit stimulus and one quality run stimulus per week, fill remaining days with easy aerobic and technique, and never stack long runs on heavy lower-body damage. Full templates and conflict resolution are in Running and CrossFit Training Balance. Expect some tradeoff at the extremes — elite Fran and elite marathon rarely coexist.

What if my gym programs hard workouts every day?

You still own intensity. Scale load and density so 2–3 days per week are truly hard and the others become technical or aerobic versions of the whiteboard. Log RPE honestly. Coaches worth training under will respect athletes who scale for stimulus — see How to Scale CrossFit Workouts.
