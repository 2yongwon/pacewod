Hero WODs Explained: Murph, DT, and How to Scale Memorial Workouts

Updated August 2026 · 13 min read

Memorial Day rolls around and every gym in the country programs Murph. Someone posts a 38-minute Rx time with a 20 lb vest. You walk in planning to "just finish" and spend 65 minutes in muscular failure, unable to walk normally for four days. The workout has a name attached to a fallen service member — and you treated it like a Tuesday metcon.

Hero WODs are not benchmarks in the Fran-and-Cindy sense. They are memorial workouts named after military personnel, firefighters, and police officers who died in service. The reps, distances, and loads carry meaning. The category demands a different mindset than chasing a leaderboard PR on a sprint benchmark.

This guide explains what Hero WODs are and why they exist, pacing strategies for Murph and DT — two of the most commonly programmed Hero workouts — how to scale with respect instead of ego, and why Hero WODs belong on special occasions, not in weekly training rotation.

What Hero WODs Are

The Hero category in CrossFit consists of workouts named in honor of deceased military, law enforcement, and first responders. Each Hero WOD carries a name, a story, and a specific workout structure chosen to reflect the person being honored. Murph honors Navy Lieutenant Michael Murphy, killed in Afghanistan in 2005. DT honors USAF Staff Sergeant Timothy P. Davis, killed by an IED in 2009.

Unlike benchmark WODs designed primarily to measure fitness progress, Hero WODs are commemorative. Gyms program them on Memorial Day, Veterans Day, 9/11 anniversaries, and the anniversary dates of specific honorees. The workout is a tribute — a deliberate hardship undertaken in memory of someone who made the ultimate sacrifice.

That context changes how you approach the session. Showing up, working hard, and finishing with integrity matters more than Rx weight or sub-40 time. Scaling is not weakness — it is participation. A partitioned Half Murph completed with full effort honors the workout better than an Rx Murph walked through with broken form and a 90-minute clock.

Expert Note

Before every Hero WOD, read or listen to the honoree's story — most gyms post it on the whiteboard or read it aloud. The workout structure is not arbitrary: Murph's mile runs and high-volume bodyweight reps reflect the kind of operational fitness Murphy trained. Knowing why the reps exist changes pacing from "survive the suffering" to "earn the memorial."

Murph: Workout Breakdown

For time:

  • 1 mile run
  • 100 pull-ups
  • 200 push-ups
  • 300 air squats
  • 1 mile run

Rx: Partition the reps as needed. Optional 20/14 lb weight vest.

Murph is a 45–75+ minute grind for most athletes — longer with a vest. It combines two running miles with 600 bodyweight reps. Grip, pressing endurance, and leg stamina all fail at different points. The athletes who finish fastest do not go out hard — they partition from rep one and hold steady splits.

Murph pacing strategy

Pre-plan your partition before the clock starts. Reactive breaking — doing pull-ups until grip fails, then resting 90 seconds — adds 10–20 minutes compared to structured sets.

  • Opening mileConservative — 30–60 sec slower than 5K race pace; save legs for 600 reps
  • Round 1–55 pull-ups / 10 push-ups / 15 squats — hold steady, no sprinting
  • Round 6–10Same partition — expect grip fatigue; do not increase rest
  • Round 11–15Push-ups break first for most — drop to 5 reps if needed, keep moving
  • Round 16–20Maintain structure; squats are usually the last movement standing
  • Closing mileShuffle if needed — any forward motion counts; vest athletes walk hills

20 rounds of 5-10-15 is the standard partition for intermediate athletes. Elites may use 20 rounds of 5-20-25 or larger sets — only if tested in training.

Murph target times by level

  • Beginner (scaled): 55–75 min — ring rows, incline push-ups, no vest
  • Intermediate (Rx bodyweight): 45–60 min — partitioned, no vest
  • Advanced (Rx): 40–50 min — efficient partition, unbroken squats in sets
  • Elite (vest): Sub-40 min with 20 lb vest — competitive territory

Estimate your opening mile with the Pace Calculator — if your easy mile is 9:00, opening Murph at 7:30 guarantees a survival shuffle on the closing mile and destroyed pull-ups in the middle.

Murph scaling options

  • Half Murph: 800m run, 50 pull-ups, 100 push-ups, 150 squats, 800m run — valid and respected entry point
  • Ring rows or jumping pull-ups: Preserve pull-up rep count; scale movement, not volume
  • Incline or knee push-ups: Full 200 reps at reduced load beats 100 Rx push-ups in sets of 3 with 2-minute rests
  • 14 lb vest instead of 20: Step toward vest Murph without full load
  • Partner Murph: Split reps evenly — common in team memorial events; each partner runs both miles

Scaling Murph is not "easier Murph" — a well-paced Half Murph at full effort produces appropriate memorial stimulus for most athletes. Rx vest Murph is a specialized test for athletes who have completed multiple no-vest Murphs and trained specifically for vest running.

DT: Workout Breakdown

5 rounds for time:

  • 12 deadlifts (155/105 lb)
  • 9 hang power cleans
  • 6 push jerks

All reps use the same barbell — a barbell complex repeated for five rounds. DT is a 8–15 minute Hero WOD for most Rx athletes, far shorter than Murph but demanding in a different way: barbell cycling under fatigue, grip endurance, and the mental grind of repeating the same complex five times without putting the bar down.

DT pacing strategy

DT rewards unbroken complexes in early rounds and smart breaking in later rounds. Going unbroken in round 1 when you have never tested unbroken DT at Rx weight is the most common pacing error.

  • Round 16-6 deadlifts / 5-4 cleans / 3-3 jerks — probe sustainable splits
  • Round 2–3Same break pattern — do not accelerate if round 1 felt easy
  • Round 4Grip and shoulders fatigue — singles on cleans acceptable
  • Round 5Finish protocol: however the bar moves forward, keep moving

Target: 90–120 seconds per round for intermediate Rx; sub-90 sec per round for advanced

DT target times by level

  • Beginner (scaled): 12–18 min at 95/65 lb or dumbbells
  • Intermediate (Rx): 8–12 min with planned breaks
  • Advanced (Rx): Sub-8 min — mostly unbroken complexes
  • Elite: Sub-5 min — touch-and-go cycling, minimal rest between rounds

DT scaling options

  • Reduce load: 115/75 lb preserves complex structure at manageable weight
  • Dumbbell complex: Same rep scheme with 50/35 lb dumbbells if barbell unavailable
  • 3 rounds instead of 5: Valid scale for athletes new to barbell cycling — note scaling in log
  • Hang cleans instead of power cleans: Reduces technical demand under fatigue

Other Notable Hero WODs

Beyond Murph and DT, gyms commonly program these Hero workouts on memorial dates:

Michael (3 rounds: 800m run, 50 back extensions, 50 sit-ups)

Shorter Hero WOD honoring Marine Lt. Michael E. Thorne. Run portions at controlled pace — 3 rounds feels manageable until round 3 back extensions burn the posterior chain. Target: 25–35 min intermediate.

Josh (21 overhead squats, 42 pull-ups, 15 power snatches — 95/65 lb)

Hero WOD with ascending/descending rep scheme. Pacing is front-loaded restraint on pull-ups — sets of 3–5 from rep one. Target: 12–20 min intermediate.

Arnie (21 Turkish get-ups each arm, 21 snatches each arm — 53/35 lb kettlebell)

Grind Hero WOD. Not a sprint. Partition TGU and snatches from the start — 3–5 reps per set. Target: 30–50 min depending on TGU proficiency.

Badger (3 rounds: 30 squat cleans, 30 pull-ups, 800m run — 95/65 lb)

Hybrid Hero combining barbell volume and running. Conservative opening 800m each round; squat cleans in sets of 3–5. Target: 25–35 min intermediate.

Scaling Hero WODs With Respect

Scaling a Hero WOD is participating in a memorial — not opting out. The community respects effort, not Rx labels. Guidelines for scaling with integrity:

  1. Scale movement, preserve intent: Ring rows instead of pull-ups, not skipping pull-ups entirely
  2. Scale load, preserve rep scheme: 95 lb deadlifts in DT, not 5 rounds of 6-4-3
  3. Scale volume for long Heroes only: Half Murph is the accepted volume scale; do not invent quarter-Murph without coach guidance
  4. Log your scaling: "Murph — Half, ring rows, incline push-ups, 42:15" is an honest score. Compare against your own scaled retests, not others' Rx times
  5. Never scale to make it easy: Hero WODs should still be hard. If you finish fresh, you scaled too much
Expert Note

On Memorial Day, gyms often see a crowd of athletes who have not trained pull-up volume in months. Coaches should pre-brief scaling options before the workout starts — not after athletes have already committed to Rx. A box that celebrates every finish at every scale creates a safer memorial culture than one where scaling carries stigma.

Why Hero WODs Are Not Daily Training

Murph once per year on Memorial Day is tradition. Murph every month is overuse. Hero WODs share characteristics that make them poor weekly programming choices:

  • High volume: Murph's 600 bodyweight reps exceed what most athletes need for adaptation in a single session — the recovery cost is 3–7 days, not 24 hours
  • High eccentric load: Push-ups and pull-ups under fatigue produce DOMS that impairs training for days
  • Memorial weight: Treating a named workout as a casual Tuesday metcon dilutes its meaning and encourages ego-driven Rx attempts
  • Running + gymnastics combinations: Hybrid Heroes like Murph and Badger stress tissues that daily programming cannot sustain at Hero intensity

Appropriate Hero WOD frequency

  • Murph: Once per year (Memorial Day) or once per training cycle as a benchmark test — not both plus random gym programming
  • DT and shorter Heroes: Quarterly at most as a fitness test
  • Training for Heroes: Build Murph capacity with EMOM pull-up work, push-up volume on the minute, and long Zone 2 runs — not by running Murph weekly

Hybrid runners combining CrossFit and endurance training need extra spacing. Murph 48 hours before a long run compromises both sessions. Schedule Hero WODs on standalone rest days from running, with 3–5 days before quality run sessions. See our running and CrossFit balance guide for integration rules.

Hero WOD Preparation

If you know Murph is coming on Memorial Day, prepare over 6–8 weeks — not the week before:

  1. Weeks 1–2: Build pull-up and push-up volume — 5 × max sets at 60% effort, 3× weekly
  2. Weeks 3–4: Practice partition — 10 rounds of 5-10-15 at moderate pace, not for time
  3. Weeks 5–6: Run 1 mile after high-rep bodyweight work — simulate Murph's structure
  4. Week 7: Half Murph for time — establish scaled or Rx baseline
  5. Week 8 (race week): Reduce volume; show up fresh on Memorial Day

For DT, practice barbell complexes in EMOM format — 1 power clean + 1 push jerk every minute for 10 minutes at workout weight builds cycling efficiency without the full Hero recovery cost.

Common Hero WOD Mistakes

  • Rx ego on memorial day: Attempting vest Murph without prior no-vest completion
  • No partition plan: Starting Murph pull-ups unbroken — grip fails at rep 30, adding 20 minutes of rest
  • Opening mile too fast: Treating Murph's first mile as a race — legs gone before squats begin
  • DT unbroken round 1: Muscular failure in round 3 when planned breaks would have saved 2 minutes total
  • Hero as weekly programming: Programming Murph monthly "for fitness" — use benchmark WODs for regular testing instead
  • Ignoring the story: Treating Hero WODs as arbitrary suffering — read the honoree's bio before the clock starts

Final Takeaway

Hero WODs are memorial workouts — named, structured tributes to fallen service members. Murph demands partition discipline and conservative opening miles. DT demands pre-planned barbell breaks and controlled complex cycling. Scale movement and load while preserving rep intent and effort.

Program Hero WODs on meaningful dates, not in weekly rotation. Prepare over weeks with targeted volume, show up with a plan, and finish with integrity. The name on the whiteboard deserves that much.

FAQ

Do I need a weight vest for Murph?

No. Rx Murph without a vest is the standard test for most athletes. The 20/14 lb vest is an additional challenge for athletes who have completed multiple no-vest Murphs with comfortable margins. First-time Murph athletes should never wear a vest. Vest Murph is a different workout — compare vest times only against prior vest attempts, not against no-vest leaderboard posts.

What is the best Murph partition strategy?

20 rounds of 5 pull-ups, 10 push-ups, 15 squats is the most common intermediate partition — it keeps sets manageable and creates a steady rhythm. Athletes with strong pull-ups may use 10 rounds of 10-20-30. Athletes with weak pull-ups should use 25 rounds of 4-8-12 to keep sets small and rest short. Decide before starting; do not change mid-workout unless grip or shoulders force it.

How long should I rest after a Hero WOD like Murph?

Expect 3–5 days before returning to high-intensity training — running intervals, heavy barbell work, or another Hero WOD. Easy movement (walking, cycling, light swimming) on days 1–2 aids recovery. DOMS peaks at 48 hours. Do not schedule a long run, race, or heavy squat session within 72 hours of Murph. Shorter Heroes like DT typically need 48 hours before quality training.

Can Hero WODs replace regular benchmark testing?

No. Hero WODs and benchmarks serve different purposes. Benchmarks (Fran, Cindy, Helen) are designed as repeatable fitness tests — short, measurable, retested every 8–12 weeks. Hero WODs are memorial events with higher volume and recovery cost. Use benchmarks for progress tracking; use Heroes for commemorative occasions. Attempting Hero WODs monthly as fitness tests produces chronic fatigue without proportional adaptation.
