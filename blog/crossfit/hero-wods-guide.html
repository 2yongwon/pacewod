Hero WODs Explained: Murph, DT, and How to Scale Memorial Workouts

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

Hero WODs honor fallen service members and first responders. They are often long, heavy, or high-volume — treat them with respect, not as random Tuesday grinders. Benchmark pacing overlaps: benchmark guide. Support training: EMOMs, WOD Generator, Pace Calculator, running balance.

Key Points

  • Murph: mile / 100 pull-ups / 200 push-ups / 300 squats / mile — partition; open mile controlled
  • DT: 5 rounds of 12 deadlift / 9 hang power clean / 6 push jerk at 155/105 — break early, same pattern each round
  • Scale with respect: reduce load/reps/volume to finish safely while keeping the workout’s character
  • Not daily training: high systemic cost — program rarely, recover fully
  • Prepare with base engine, pull-up/push-up volume, and barbell cycling — not hero WODs every week

Pacing Snapshots

  • Opening mileConservative — 30–60 sec slower than 5K race pace; save legs for 600 reps
  • Round 1–55 pull-ups / 10 push-ups / 15 squats — hold steady, no sprinting
  • Round 6–10Same partition — expect grip fatigue; do not increase rest
  • Round 11–15Push-ups break first for most — drop to 5 reps if needed, keep moving
  • Round 16–20Maintain structure; squats are usually the last movement standing
  • Closing mileShuffle if needed — any forward motion counts; vest athletes walk hills

20 rounds of 5-10-15 is the standard partition for intermediate athletes. Elites may use 20 rounds of 5-20-25 or larger sets — only if tested in training.

  • Round 16-6 deadlifts / 5-4 cleans / 3-3 jerks — probe sustainable splits
  • Round 2–3Same break pattern — do not accelerate if round 1 felt easy
  • Round 4Grip and shoulders fatigue — singles on cleans acceptable
  • Round 5Finish protocol: however the bar moves forward, keep moving

Target: 90–120 seconds per round for intermediate Rx; sub-90 sec per round for advanced

Practical Takeaway

Show up prepared, scale honestly, and race a plan you can finish with quality. The tribute is completing the work safely — not collecting an injury.

FAQ

Is a vest required for Murph?

No. Many athletes go vestless or use a lighter load. Log the conditions so future retests compare cleanly.

How should I recover afterward?

Easy movement, sleep, protein, and 48+ hours before the next hard lower-body or long-run session for most athletes.
