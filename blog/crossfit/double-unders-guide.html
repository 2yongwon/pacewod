Double Unders for CrossFit: Progressions, Fatigue Management, and WOD Strategy

Updated August 2026 · 14 min read

Double unders look like a coordination trick until they show up for the 80th rep in a metcon. Then they become a calf, shoulder, and composure test. Athletes who can string 50 unbroken when fresh often fragment into singles after thrusters — not because the skill vanished, but because fatigue management never entered the plan.

This guide covers the single → double progression, trip-up recovery, unbroken versus planned sets in WODs, and calf fatigue specifics. Strategy changes by format — see the AMRAP Workouts Guide and For Time Workouts Guide for clock context, and use movement scaling guidance when doubles are not yet reliable. Rope, Setup, and Baseline Standards

Before progressions, fix equipment. A rope that is 6 inches too long forces wide elbows and late spins; too short clips toes and teaches panic jumps.

  • Length check: Stand on the rope midpoint; handles should reach roughly armpit to lower rib height (brand and handle style vary — tune until doubles clear without shoulder shrugging).
  • Cable: Speed cable for skill and WODs once you clear 20+ doubles; beaded ropes slow feedback for absolute beginners but help some athletes see rhythm.
  • Surface: Wood or rubber. Concrete destroys cables and ankles; thick mats deaden timing.
  • Shoes: Stable trainers beat soft maximal cushion for timing.

Baseline test (fresh): max unbroken doubles, and time for 50 doubles. Log both monthly. Example benchmarks:

  • Early intermediate: 15–25 unbroken · 50 doubles in 0:45–1:15
  • Solid intermediate: 40–60 unbroken · 50 in 0:30–0:45
  • Advanced: 80+ unbroken · 50 in sub-0:30 · 100 in sub-1:00

Single → Double Progression

Skip the "just try doubles" method if you trip every third rep. Use stages with numeric exit criteria.

Stage 1 — Single Under Efficiency

Goal: 100 singles at consistent bounce, quiet feet, wrists spinning (not shoulders). Cadence target ≈ 160–180 turns/min. If singles are pogo-stomps with bent knees flaring, doubles will not stick.

Exit criteria: 2 minutes continuous singles without a trip, heart rate under control (conversational possible).

Stage 2 — Single-Single-Double

Pattern: two singles, one double, repeat. Perform 10 successful doubles inside the pattern. Rest as needed. This teaches the higher jump and faster wrist speed without demanding unbroken doubles.

Exit criteria: 20 doubles accumulated via the pattern in under 3 minutes of work.

Stage 3 — Double Attempts in Small Sets

Attempt sets of 3–5 doubles. Stop one rep before the usual trip. Rest 20–40 seconds. Accumulate 50–75 total doubles per practice.

Exit criteria: 3 sets of 10 unbroken in a session.

Stage 4 — Capacity

Build to 25, 40, then 50 unbroken. Add fatigue only after fresh 50 is repeatable twice in a week: e.g., 15 thrusters + 30 doubles × 3 sets as a skill couplet — not a hero WOD.

Expert Note

Wrist speed fixes more failed doubles than "jump higher." If the rope catches your toes, film from the side: many athletes jump high enough but spin late. Cue "spin first, then jump" for one session and count successful reps. Athletes often gain 10–15 unbroken within two weeks from timing alone — without any calf-strength miracle.

Trip-Up Recovery Under the Clock

Tripping is inevitable in WODs. The score difference is recovery time, not whether you trip.

Bad Recovery (Common)

  • Trip → emotional reset → rope tangle → re-find handles → stare at the clock → restart too hot → trip again
  • Cost: 8–15 seconds per incident

Good Recovery Protocol

  1. Stop the bounce immediately (do not keep hopping while tangled).
  2. Open the rope with one practiced motion — rehearse this when fresh.
  3. Two breaths, shoulders down.
  4. Restart at 80% wrist speed for 3–5 reps, then resume target pace.

Target recovery: 3–5 seconds from trip to first successful double. Practice 10 intentional trip-and-reset drills weekly. Athletes who train resets beat "never trip" athletes who freeze for 12 seconds when they finally do.

Numeric impact: In a For Time workout with 120 doubles, three trips at 12 seconds each = 36 seconds lost. Same three trips at 4 seconds each = 12 seconds lost. That is often a full place on the whiteboard.

Unbroken vs Sets in WODs

Unbroken is not automatically fastest. Calculate before you chalk up.

Decision Framework

Let U = your reliable unbroken doubles when fresh. In a WOD, assume capacity ≈ 50–70% of U after another fatiguing movement, and ≈ 40–60% of U late in a long AMRAP.

  • If the set is ≤ 50% of U, go unbroken.
  • If the set is 50–80% of U, plan one quick break (e.g., 40-20 for 60).
  • If the set is >80% of U or you are already breathing hard, use smaller sets from the start.

Worked Examples

Athlete with 60 unbroken fresh:

  • 30 doubles after row: unbroken (50% of fresh)
  • 50 doubles in Annie-style (50-40-30-20-10): break 50s as 30-20; try unbroken 40s only if still composed
  • 100 doubles cash-out: 40-30-30 or 25×4 with 5-breath rests — not death-march unbroken attempt

Time math for 100 doubles:

  • Unbroken attempt that fails at 55, then messy singles: often 1:45–2:15 total
  • Planned 25-25-25-25 with 5-second rests: 4 × ~12–15 sec work + 15 sec rest ≈ 1:10–1:25 for many intermediates

Planned sets win when unbroken reliability under fatigue is low. Ego unbroken wins only when you have tested it after similar fatigue.

Format Strategy: AMRAP vs For Time

AMRAP

Doubles every round punish early max sets. Example: AMRAP 12 — 15 power cleans + 30 double unders.

  • Target 6 rounds → 120 seconds/round
  • Budget 55–65 sec cleans + 25–35 sec doubles
  • Doubles: unbroken 30 if reliable; else 20-10 with a 3-second rest

If doubles take 50+ seconds in round 1, you will miss the round target — scale volume or use singles factor (below). Round consistency rules from the AMRAP guide apply: keep drift ≤5–8%.

For Time

Chipper-style and couplet For Time pieces reward pre-written break plans. Write "50 DUs = 30-20" on your whiteboard note. Do not invent breaks after the first trip. See For Time break planning for segment logging.

Calf Fatigue and Elastic Management

Double unders are reactive calf work. High volume without preparation produces next-day stiffness that ruins running and box jumps.

Why Calves Blow Up

  • Too much plantarflexion stiffness with minimal knee bend (good for timing, costly for tissue)
  • Sudden jump from 50 DUs/week to 300 DUs in one Hero WOD
  • Fatigue-induced heavy landings (heels slamming)

Management Numbers

  • Weekly DU volume (practice + WODs): increase ≤20–25% week over week when building skill
  • Hard DU metcon: max 1× per day; avoid DU-dense WODs on consecutive days during skill blocks
  • Pre-fatigue cue: if vertical jump feel is gone and landings get loud, switch to planned singles or reduce set size — form loss precedes soft-tissue complaint

Simple prep (3–4 minutes): 20 single-leg calf raises/side, 30 seconds pogo hops, 20 singles, 10 doubles. Not a full warm-up — a tissue primer.

Hybrid runners: place DU-heavy metcons at least 24–36 hours away from quality track sessions or long runs. Calves cannot be springy for both on back-to-back days without cost. See Running and CrossFit Training Balance.

Expert Note

Shoulders and traps often fail before calves in high-rep doubles because athletes "muscle" the rope with big arm circles. Cue pencil-thin elbows, pinkies heavy, rotation from wrists. If traps are on fire at rep 40, film yourself — oversized arm circles add both fatigue and timing variance. Fixing this often drops 50-double time by 5–10 seconds without any calf adaptation.

Scaling Doubles Without Losing the Workout

Scaling is not failure; mismatched scaling is. Options ranked by stimulus preservation:

  1. Single unders at 2× or 3× reps (30 DUs → 60–90 singles) when practicing timing but lacking unbroken doubles
  2. Attempted doubles with a cap (e.g., 45 seconds of DU attempts) then move on — use in classes carefully so scores stay comparable
  3. Penguin taps or penguin jumps for athletes building wrist speed without rope confidence
  4. Plate penguin or lateral line hops only when rope work is not available — weaker transfer

Avoid "heavy singles as slow jogs" for 3× reps that turn a 90-second station into 4 minutes of calf grind. Match intended time domain. Broader load and skill scaling principles sit in our CrossFit scaling guide.

FAQ

How long does it take to learn double unders?

With 3 focused sessions weekly, many athletes go from zero to 10–20 unbroken in 3–6 weeks. Reaching workout-durable 50s often takes 2–4 months depending on coordination history and calf resilience. Daily frustrated attempts without a progression usually slow learning — short, quality practice wins.

Should I do singles or scale reps when I cannot double under yet?

Use 2×–3× single unders when the workout's intent is monostructural density and you can match the time domain. If the class is competing on the whiteboard, ask the coach for the gym's standard. Do not grind 5× singles that turn calves to stone and miss the metabolic point of the piece.

Why do I have doubles when fresh but not in WODs?

Fresh doubles use calm timing. Mid-WOD doubles compete with elevated heart rate, grip fatigue, and rushed resets. Train doubles after a small dose of thrusters, burpees, or rowing — 3 sets of 10 thrusters + 30 DU attempts teaches transferable skill. Also lower your unbroken ambitions to 50–70% of fresh max inside metcons.

Do double unders ruin my running calves?

Sudden spikes can. Progressive DU volume plus spacing away from hard runs usually plays fine and can even improve stiffness for sprinting. Warning signs: morning calf tenderness, loss of pop on strides, or pain altering your run gait — cut DU volume 40–50% for a week and resume with smaller sets.
